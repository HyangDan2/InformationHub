## Company and position in the value chain

CXMT (`688825.SH`) began in Hefei in June 2016 and is the only company in mainland China that mass-produces general-purpose DRAM. It runs an integrated-device-manufacturer model — design, fabrication and sales in one company — with three 12-inch fabs across Hefei and Beijing. On fourth-quarter 2025 sales it held <strong>7.67%</strong> of the global market, first in China and fourth worldwide. It is the only new entrant in nearly three decades into a market where Samsung, SK hynix and Micron shared more than 90%.

What it sells is straightforward: DDR4 and DDR5, LPDDR4X and LPDDR5/5X, and now the beginnings of HBM3. In 2025, 99% of revenue came from DDR and LPDDR. The LPDDR family has supplied more than 66% of revenue since 2022, and the products go into servers, phones, PCs and cars. Domestic sales were 42.79% of 2025 revenue; the rest went to overseas module makers and OEMs.

In the value chain it sits close to the wafer. Upstream are lithography, deposition and etch tools plus materials, with domestic suppliers such as NAURA, AMEC, Piotech and ACM Research on the equipment side and companies like Jiangfeng, Yoke and Tianshan on materials. Downstream are OSATs such as Tongfu Microelectronics and module makers including Longsys, Biwin and Dimensity. End demand is the domestic AI chip camp represented by Huawei and Cambricon, plus global phone, PC and server OEMs. HBM sits at the far end of that structure.

Capacity is being built for next year, not this one. Wafer output of about 320,000 a month rises to 350,000 by end-2026, 420,000 in 2027 and 500,000 in 2028, with targets of double by 2030 and triple by 2035. By end-2026 it will be close to Micron's roughly 385,000, and in 2027 it will hold about 17% of global DRAM wafer capacity. Its bit share goes from 9% in 2025 to 12% in 2027.

The listing was four months ago. At CNY 8.66 a share it sold 6.688bn shares and raised CNY 57.919bn (CNY 66.607bn with the greenshoe). On July 27 it closed its first day at CNY 49.00, up <strong>465.82%</strong>, with a market value of CNY 3.28tn that made it China's most valuable listed company, and its CNY 141.187bn of first-day turnover was a record for a single A-share. The stock now trades at CNY 33.60 — 3.9 times the offer price, but 31% below that first close.

## Recent results and key metrics

Third-quarter revenue was <strong>CNY 118.714bn</strong> (+613.18%) and net profit attributable to shareholders was <strong>CNY 54.435bn</strong>. Revenue rose 19.30% sequentially while profit rose only 3.01%. Consolidated net profit increased 19% to CNY 88.98bn, but minority interests reached CNY 34.55bn, or 39% of the total, which held back the attributable figure. That reflects the ownership structure of the newly ramped Hefei phase-two and Beijing lines.

The number that stands out is the margin. Gross margin reached <strong>89.53%</strong> in Q3, up 1.94 percentage points from Q2. For all of 2025 it was 41.02%, so it has more than doubled in a year. Micron reported 86.8% and SK hynix 86.0% in the same quarter, making CXMT the industry's most profitable DRAM maker. Prices rose while unit costs fell on scale, and the Q3 report attributed the gain to higher volumes and prices alongside lower unit costs from larger production.

For the nine months, revenue was <strong>CNY 269.024bn</strong> (+738.51%) and net profit <strong>CNY 132.040bn</strong>, against a CNY 5.280bn loss a year earlier. Excluding non-recurring items, profit was CNY 134.030bn. Operating cash flow reached CNY 254.751bn, up 1,864.52%, and research spending rose 73.03% to CNY 11.071bn. In nine months the company generated 4.4 times its entire 2025 revenue of CNY 61.799bn and 70 times its 2025 net profit of CNY 1.875bn.

The balance sheet grew with it. Total assets reached CNY 672.535bn at the end of September, up 99.69% from the start of the year, shareholders' equity rose 352.62% to CNY 256.876bn and cash stood at CNY 285.165bn. The CNY 36.65bn of accumulated losses carried into 2025 has largely been earned back. But with property, plant and construction in progress rising sharply, depreciation begins to hit the income statement in earnest next year.

The source of the margin is visible in the price data. Conventional DRAM contract prices rose 93–98% sequentially in Q1 2026, 58–63% in Q2 and 13–18% in Q3. Revenue going from CNY 50.8bn in Q1 to CNY 118.714bn in Q3 was a price story more than a volume story. That is why the ceiling on the margin is set by contract prices, not by the company.

## Today's move and the competitive picture

On November 25 CXMT opened at <strong>CNY 35.00</strong>, touched 35.38 and slid to <strong>33.50</strong>, closing at CNY 33.60 (-7.34%) on CNY 9.483bn of turnover and a 6.13% turnover ratio. Flows were the worst in the market: main funds sold <strong>CNY 1.791bn</strong> net, the largest of any stock, ahead of GigaDevice at CNY 906m. That single name accounted for about 12% of the CNY 14.499bn that left the electronics sector.

Three pressures hit at once. First, Micron fell 10.97% and SanDisk 11% overnight in the US. Second, TrendForce cut its 2027 forecasts, lowering NAND revenue to USD 419.3bn and DRAM to USD 1.15tn as Chinese cloud providers reduced orders, and projected narrowing quarterly DRAM increases that level off in the second half of 2027; a separate report the same day raised the risk of a broad contract price decline in 2028. Third, MOFCOM's suspension of part of its US export controls through January weakened the urgency of domestic substitution. That is why optical modules rose while domestic chips fell.

The company's answer was its buyback. On October 30 it proposed CNY 1–2bn; on November 19 it raised the programme to <strong>CNY 2.5–5.0bn</strong> with a CNY 63.78 cap. Of that, CNY 1.5–3.0bn is for value support and will be cancelled in full, and with the CNY 500m–1.0bn earmarked for capital reduction up to CNY 4.0bn of stock could disappear. The company acted after its November 6 close of CNY 40.41 met the rule for a 20% decline over 20 sessions. Execution still requires approval at the December 9 extraordinary shareholder meeting, and cancellation needs a creditor-notice process.

Competition is tilting CXMT's way. Most of Samsung's 2027–2029 capacity is contracted, with some customers seeking extensions to 2031. SK hynix has long-term agreements with 14 customers and is effectively sold out through 2029, and even Apple is negotiating a long-term DRAM deal with Samsung. Major suppliers are meeting only 60–70% of demand. This is a market short of volume, not just price, and CXMT is the fourth supplier filling the gap.

HBM is the harder question. SK hynix holds 58% of the global market, and CXMT's HBM3 is still stabilising 8-high stacking, two to three generations behind. SemiAnalysis estimates front-end yield of about 35%, back-end of 70% and an overall figure near 25%. A wafer yields roughly a third as many bits in HBM as in conventional DRAM, so at today's prices conventional DRAM is far more profitable. The company is therefore raising HBM allocation gradually — from 5,000 wafers a month to 30,000 by end-2026, 55,000 in 2027 and 100,000 in 2028 — with a 2028 HBM revenue target of USD 2bn. Demand comes almost entirely from Huawei, Cambricon and the domestic AI chip camp.

The technology path takes a detour. The workhorse is the G4 node (1z-class) and the next step, G5 (1a-class), is designed to work without EUV lithography. In the Q3 report the company said a DRAM sample on its sixth-generation process platform had completed engineering validation, and that LPDDR6 samples had shipped to customers. Going advanced without EUV costs yield and unit cost; for now, extraordinary pricing covers the difference.
