## Market

The Shanghai Composite closed at <strong>3,606.49 (+3.73%)</strong>, reclaiming 3,600 for the first time in weeks. It opened at 3,482.38, traded as low as 3,467.41 and as high as 3,610.72, and spent the morning drifting near 3,480 before turning vertical just after midday. The Shenzhen Component rose <strong>7.24%</strong> to 10,814.02, ChiNext <strong>11.61%</strong> to 2,443.96, the STAR 50 <strong>15.07%</strong> to 1,346.25, the STAR Composite <strong>14.21%</strong> to 1,489.77 and the BSE 50 <strong>13.20%</strong> to 851.79. The CSI 300 added 4.17% to 4,063.06 and the SSE 50 2.94% to 2,834.68. ChiNext's gain was its largest since October 8, 2024; the STAR 50's was its third-largest ever, behind September 30 and October 8, 2024.

Breadth matched the headline. Gainers outnumbered decliners 5,382 to 181, with 96% of stocks higher, 426 limit-ups and one limit-down. Turnover across Shanghai and Shenzhen was <strong>CNY 1.9998tn</strong>, up <strong>CNY 682.4bn</strong> from the previous session. Shanghai traded CNY 929.8bn (from CNY 613.3bn) and Shenzhen CNY 1.0700tn (from CNY 704.0bn); including Beijing the total reached CNY 2.0173tn. Turnover that had fallen for three straight sessions doubled in a day.

The trigger came at midday from the State Council. An executive meeting on capital-market investment and financing reform said it would keep improving the market's stabilisation mechanism, give fuller play to the "national team" and <strong>further optimise Central Huijin's quasi-stabilisation-fund function to ensure ample liquidity</strong>. It also called for more dividends and buybacks, better policies to channel insurers, enterprise annuities and the social-security fund into equities, stronger defences against imported risk, and full-chain supervision. It was the second time in a month that the cabinet had promised to improve the stabilisation mechanism, after November 6, and the first time it named the stabilisation-fund role. The line that moved prices was the promise to "devise incremental policies" as well as implement existing ones. Neither the size nor the mechanism has been disclosed; the Central Economic Work Conference in December is the next checkpoint.

Semiconductors led the afternoon. The sector rose <strong>14.97%</strong> on CNY 280.4bn of turnover; electronic chemicals added 14.53%, communications equipment 11.46%, software 11.42%, IT services 11.36%, components 11.22%, automation equipment 11.19% and consumer electronics 10.21%. Eighty-nine sectors rose and only banks fell (-1.07%). High-dividend names such as China Shenhua, Sinopec and China Mobile gave back morning gains while growth stocks took the money. Main funds bought <strong>CNY 23.824bn</strong> of semiconductors and sold banks, agricultural processing and white goods. By theme, chip concepts drew CNY 63.47bn, data centres CNY 42.85bn, Huawei-related names CNY 41.71bn and semiconductor equipment CNY 3.2bn. At the stock level Zhongji Innolight (+CNY 3.769bn), Eoptolink (+CNY 2.921bn), CXMT (+CNY 2.475bn), Shengyi (+CNY 1.551bn) and GigaDevice (+CNY 1.254bn) led.

Within chips, the equipment chain had news of its own. Jingce Electronics disclosed after the close on November 26 that a subsidiary had signed a CNY 199.8m contract to supply front-end metrology tools. On November 27, reports said new orders at AMEC, ACM Research and peers had hit records in the first nine months, and that <strong>domestic tools had taken more than half of purchases on the third-phase line of a leading memory maker for the first time</strong>. ACM's backlog was CNY 17.073bn at end-September, up 88.20% year on year. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology approved the industry's first dedicated quality-management standard for semiconductor equipment and components. SEMI expects global 300mm front-end equipment spending of USD 149bn in 2026, up 31%. National Bureau of Statistics data for January-October showed electronics profits up 1.0x, contributing 9.7 percentage points to industrial profit growth. Orders, standards and profits were confirmed by numbers on the same day.

Policy and export controls pointed the same way. On November 26 Guangdong published its 15th five-year plan for compute, targeting 1,000 EFlops (FP16) of intelligent computing by 2028, encouraging compute centres to prioritise domestic chips and to build out the CANN ecosystem and Ascend operators. The same day the Financial Times reported that Nvidia had told Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, following the US interim final rule published on November 19. If the route through Southeast Asian data centres is closed, the argument goes, demand shifts back to domestic compute and domestic chips and tools. Domestic AI chips, equipment, optical modules and PCBs all rose together.

Asia was already rebounding. New York was closed overnight for Thanksgiving and shut early at 1pm on November 27, leaving little external direction. Even so the Kospi rose <strong>4.34%</strong> to 4,915.68, reclaiming 4,900 for the first time in three sessions, and the Kosdaq jumped <strong>8.13%</strong> to 708.99 with a buy-side sidecar triggered at 10:22am. Foreigners bought KRW 1.13tn of Kospi shares, Samsung Electronics rose 4.55% and SK Hynix 5.41%. The Nikkei added 2.11% to 58,711.11 and Kioxia 8.86%. Hong Kong followed, with the Hang Seng +2.11%, the HSCEI +2.01% and the Hang Seng Tech index +5.45%. The won closed at 1,337.9 per dollar, up 4.8.

Every one of our eleven names rose. Five hit their caps: Eoptolink, Piotech and VeriSilicon at +20%, Shengyi and GigaDevice at +10%. Zhongji Innolight added 19.98%, Cambricon 18.68%, Hygon 18.05%, Montage 15.69%, SMIC 15.38% and CXMT 12.97%. Together the eleven traded <strong>CNY 148.1bn</strong>, 7.4% of the whole market. Zhongji Innolight took both the turnover crown (CNY 33.397bn) and the largest main-fund inflow (CNY 3.769bn); CXMT turned over 11.75% of its float, the most in the group. At the other end Hygon (1.85%) and Cambricon (2.85%) traded thinly relative to their size and share price.
