## The company and its place in the chain

Zhongji Innolight (中际旭创, `300308.SZ`) makes high-speed optical transceivers for data centres. It became a module maker in 2017 by absorbing Suzhou InnoLight, and is now one of the two largest in the world. On July 30, 2026 it also listed in Hong Kong (3308.HK), raising HK$60.9bn including the greenshoe. Combined A- and H-share capital stood at 1.178bn shares as of November 20.

What it sells is the part that turns light into electricity and back. Thousands of accelerators in one rack cannot pass data to each other over copper at the distance and speed required; 800G and 1.6T modules fill that gap. In the first half of 2026, 99.0% of revenue came from optical modules, and the mix kept shifting toward 800G and 1.6T. The company also designs its own silicon photonics chips, a choice that has become one of the drivers of this year's margin expansion.

In the chain, this company sits at **optical interconnect**. Above it are DSPs (Broadcom, Marvell), EML and CW light sources, and silicon photonics; below it are the hyperscalers — Nvidia, Google, Microsoft, Amazon and Meta are the names most often cited, though the company does not disclose per-customer orders. What is disclosed is concentration: 94.8% of first-half revenue came from overseas (CNY 39.615bn), with the US roughly 60%. On 2025 disclosures, the top five customers were 81.9% of revenue and the largest single customer 25.1%.

On 2025 numbers, module sales were USD 5.333bn, a 21–23% global share, with about 40% in 800G and more than 50% in 1.6T. Share rises with each generation, because faster parts are harder to qualify and to yield, and only a few suppliers have already crossed that line. The advantage is real but conditional: it weakens the moment a customer adds a second source to save money. That is precisely why the stock fell 12.69% in a single day in early November on order-cut rumours.

## Recent results and key metrics

Q3 revenue was **CNY 27.769bn** (+171.83%) and attributable net profit **CNY 8.587bn** (+173.74%), both records, with revenue up 24.63% sequentially. The quarterly path ran CNY 19.496bn, CNY 22.282bn and CNY 27.769bn — roughly 20% growth each quarter. Profit growth of 8.5% in Q3 looks slower than Q2's 38%, but Q3 carried a CNY 440m fair-value loss on equity investments and a CNY 254m FX loss related to the Hong Kong proceeds. Excluding both, adjusted profit was CNY 9.018bn, up 22.3% sequentially — almost exactly the revenue growth rate.

For the nine months, revenue reached **CNY 69.547bn** (+178.13%) and net profit **CNY 22.238bn** (+211.81%). Against 2025's full-year CNY 38.240bn of revenue and CNY 10.797bn of profit, nine months is already 1.8× and 2.1× respectively. The company's stated 2026 revenue target was CNY 100bn; it has booked 70% of that with a quarter to spare, and roughly CNY 30bn in Q4 would close the gap.

Margin is the metric that matters most here. Q3 gross margin was **46.61%**, up 3.82pp year on year and 0.19pp sequentially, and 46.39% for nine months. Management gives three reasons: a richer high-end mix, in-house silicon photonics cutting bill-of-materials cost, and better yields and productivity. Q3 selling prices were broadly flat versus the first half, so the improvement came from mix and cost, not price — which is exactly why its durability needs separate testing.

On the balance sheet, inventory and receivables stand out. At the end of Q3, inventory was **CNY 23.878bn** (+88.3% from the start of the year), receivables **CNY 17.309bn** (+175.8%), prepayments CNY 1.567bn (+1,068%) and construction in progress CNY 5.495bn (+286.5%). The company attributes all of it to a growing order book. Nine-month operating cash flow was CNY 8.008bn, positive, and Q3 alone was CNY 6.209bn after a deficit in Q2. But that is 36% of net profit. Working capital outrunning revenue is common in a company growing this fast; if Q4 does not convert that inventory into sales, the quality of the earnings becomes the question.

Capital allocation arrived on the same day. The board approved a CNY 2–4bn buyback at a ceiling of CNY 600 a share, over three months, for employee incentives — up to 6.66m shares, 0.57% of the register. Against total assets of CNY 129.755bn and equity of CNY 94.363bn at the end of September, the amount is modest. The signal is not: the company has written down that it will pay 44% above the current CNY 417.20.

## Today's issue and the competitive picture

On November 23 the stock closed at **CNY 417.20 (+6.10%)**, reaching 425.00 intraday, alongside an optical-module sector that opened around CNY 165 and ran. Turnover of **CNY 22.767bn** was the highest in the entire A-share market. Only two stocks cleared CNY 20bn — this one and Eoptolink — and together they took CNY 43.3bn, or 2.8% of the market's total traded value.

There were two catalysts. The first was the company's own: at 9:30 a.m. the board approved the buyback that chairman Liu Sheng had proposed on November 18, with a CNY 600 cap 44% above the close. The second was sector-wide. After the Global Electronics Association's PCB shortage warning, reports said Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro were asking 25–45% more for AI-server orders already signed. Higher server prices read as evidence that component demand and pricing were holding. Tianfu Communication's 13.82% and Eoptolink's 9.92% came from the same place.

The competitive numbers are these. Q3 revenue was CNY 27.769bn at Zhongji against CNY 10.940bn at Eoptolink — 2.5 times. Direction matters more: Zhongji's revenue grew 24.6% sequentially while Eoptolink's fell 12.98%. Profit split the same way: +8.5% QoQ at Zhongji, −19.07% QoQ at Eoptolink (though +61.15% year on year). The likely explanation is that 1.6T volumes scaled in 2026, separating the names with more 1.6T exposure from those still weighted to 800G. Eoptolink's gross margin of about 47.7% is slightly higher than Zhongji's 46.61%, reflecting different product and customer mixes.

The market is asking two questions. First, are the 2027 orders actually firm? Second, do they still carry today's margin in 2027? On the first, the company says firm POs cover almost all of 2027 and some customers have given 2028 guidance; at the November 3 briefing it said a few customers had shifted from 800G to 1.6T but nothing had been cancelled. On the second, it would only say that Q3 prices were flat versus the first half and that 2027 quotes looked rational. The numbers arrive with the Q4 and Q1 reports.

One thing should be said plainly. Monday's 6.10% was a buyback and a sector bounce arriving together, and against the August peak in the CNY 940s the stock is still less than half. With doubts about AI capex persistence unresolved, the distance between the price the company will pay (up to CNY 600) and the price the market gives it (CNY 417.20) is where this company currently stands.
