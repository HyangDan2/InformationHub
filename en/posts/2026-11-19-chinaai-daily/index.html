China AI Daily Report · November 19

With Shanghai down 0.28%, STAR 50 rose 1.83% to 1,270.78 on its own. New BIS export controls taking effect and a 'Samsung foundry audit' rumour drove intraday volatility.

The two optical-module names took first and second place; turnover is ranked by each name's own traded value.

The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,577.62 (−0.28%), the Shenzhen Component at 10,583.20 (−0.71%) and ChiNext at 2,293.34 (−0.79%), but the STAR 50 held its ground alone at 1,270.78 (+1.83%). The Shenzhen Component set a fresh low in more than a year. Turnover across the A-share market was CNY 1.3146tn, CNY 65.4bn less than the previous day, and more than 4,800 names fell. CNY 3.05bn flowed into the semiconductor sector on a net basis, with institutional money moving into memory chips, stocks held by the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund (the Big Fund) and the CPO theme.

Two things drove the session. First, the US Commerce Department's new BIS export controls — remote access to AI chips from overseas data centres, exports through intermediary countries, and licensing for leading-edge foundry work — took effect officially with publication in the Federal Register. Combined with expectations for domestic substitution that had grown around the US–China summit at APEC in Gyeongju, foundry and equipment names jumped. CITIC Securities said China's semiconductor equipment localisation rate has entered a genuine upswing and forecast equipment orders rising from roughly CNY 90bn in 2025 to CNY 150–200bn in 2026.

Second, a rumour that spread overnight — that "Samsung has started a forensic audit (穿透严查) of foundry work for Chinese chip designers" knocked AI chips down early. After checking with several foundries and design houses, Shanghai Securities News reported that it was not true, and the market recovered much of the loss in the afternoon. Breadth was thin: 4,100-odd names fell on the 18th even as the indices bounced, and more than 4,800 fell today.

688256 · Cambricon

It opened on a friendly note as Nvidia's formal halt of H200 shipments to China was read as demand shifting to domestic alternatives, but the "Samsung audit" rumour shook AI chips across the board and the stock fell to CNY 876.00 (−7.7%) intraday. It then recovered almost all of that to close at CNY 934.00 (−1.58%). Its CNY 12.80bn of turnover was third across the whole A-share market.

688041 · Hygon

This was the epicentre of the day's volatility. The rumour pushed it to CNY 156.05 intraday, a 17% fall against the previous close, before the rumour was denied and the company confirmed that "supply and operations are stable"; it then rebounded sharply in the afternoon to close at CNY 180.00 (−4.81%) — more than 15% off the intraday low. The business, the numbers and the competitive picture are set out in more depth in a separate spotlight → Hygon spotlight.

300308 · Zhongji Innolight

Overnight, chairman Liu Sheng formally proposed a buyback of CNY 2–4bn, citing the need to respond to sharp price moves and his confidence in long-term value. The stock rose to CNY 413.77 intraday before closing at CNY 408.40 (+3.05%), and its CNY 16.61bn of turnover was the highest in the entire A-share market.

300502 · Eoptolink

In a filing overnight the company stated flatly that "the order backlog is sufficient and there is no demand reduction or order cutting whatsoever", that its 1.6T optical modules have entered large-scale production and shipment, and that it had completed a CNY 800m buyback (4.216m shares). The stock fell to CNY 152.00 early before closing at CNY 167.06 (+5.59%), with CNY 14.63bn of turnover ranking second in the market.

688981 · SMIC

Expectations for domestic substitution, plus talk of a 2027 foundry price increase, lifted it to CNY 122.25 (+6.21%), leading the STAR 50 higher. Turnover was CNY 10.50bn. China Fund News attributed the strength in SMIC and Hua Hong Grace to expectations that AI compute demand in China and calls for supply-chain security will drive upgrades of local foundry lines. DIGITIMES reported that SMIC's third-quarter foundry revenue (USD 3.19bn) has closed the gap to second-placed Samsung (estimated USD 3.02–3.18bn) sharply.

600183 · Shengyi Technology

CNY 60.29 (−1.00%). There was no new filing on the day. In its Nov 18 announcement (2026-075) the company said that after cumulative declines of more than 20% over Nov 13, 16 and 17 there was "no material information undisclosed by the company or by shareholders holding 5% or more, and both production and orders remain normal". The same filing reconfirmed that a CNY 200–300m buyback begun on Oct 22 is under way. Industry talk since November has suggested CCL prices for M6 and below rising 15% and adhesive sheets (PP) 25%, but the company has not confirmed those figures. Subsidiary Shengyi Electronics (688183) reported third-quarter revenue of CNY 3.764bn (+23.00%) and net profit of CNY 801m (+37.10%).

688072 · Piotech

News that the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology approved its Semiconductor Equipment and Components Quality Management System standard, together with forecasts for domestic equipment orders, took the stock to CNY 547.50 (+4.75%). The intraday high was CNY 550.00. No company-specific filing was found for the day.

603986 · GigaDevice

The company executed CNY 50.05m (231,000 shares) of buybacks on Nov 18. The outlook for a continuing NOR and SLC NAND shortage, and for expansion in edge-AI memory, is unchanged. The stock closed at CNY 218.81 (−0.34%), essentially flat.

688008 · Montage Technology

It completed a second CNY 500m buyback (3.314m shares) in full, 20 days after starting it (average price CNY 150.88). On the business side, the company began mass production of its fifth-generation DDR5 RCD chip in September and says the share of DDR5 third- to fifth-generation shipments is rising sharply. The stock was effectively unchanged at CNY 137.60 (−0.14%).

688521 · VeriSilicon

The company officially launched Nano-XR, a low-power wearable IP subsystem for next-generation AI and AR smart glasses. The rumour still dragged the stock to CNY 79.50 (−12%) intraday and it closed at CNY 83.09 (−8.09%), the steepest fall of the eleven names.

688825 · CXMT

CNY 37.50 (+1.60%). The financial improvement drew attention: third-quarter gross margin of 89.53% (+1.94pp quarter on quarter), a debt ratio down to 36.34%, and CNY 285.17bn of cash and equivalents. Third-quarter revenue was CNY 118.71bn (+613% YoY), the shortage of DDR5 for AI servers is continuing into the fourth quarter, and a plan for LPDDR6 mass production was set out.

What to Watch Next

  1. 01
    Policy

    How the new BIS export controls are actually enforced

    Watch how the leading-edge foundry licensing and intermediary-country rules work through domestic design houses and foundry order books. The first observation point is the actual processing time for licence applications.

    Effective now · watch Commerce Department notices
  2. 02
    Pricing

    Whether the 2027 foundry price rises are confirmed

    If the 10–20% increase that has only been industry chatter shows up in customer notices or guidance, it becomes the basis for raising SMIC and equipment earnings estimates.

    Around the December results season
  3. 03
    Risk

    A repeat of the 'Samsung audit' rumour

    Unverified chatter like today's can produce an intraday collapse. Read filings and company confirmations first, and do not change positions on a rumour alone.

    At any time
  4. 04
    Memory

    CXMT's LPDDR6 ramp and whether the DDR5 shortage lasts

    This feeds directly into memory price negotiations and demand for domestic modules and interfaces (Montage). The key is whether quarterly guidance fixes a mass-production date.

    Fourth-quarter guidance
  5. 05
    Flows

    Support once the buybacks are finished

    Both Montage and Eoptolink have completed their programmes. Watch for weaker downside support as the buyback bid disappears, and for any new programme being announced.

    2–4 weeks after completion
  6. 06
    Calendar

    The next results and guidance window

    Until the December results season, policy headlines are likely to drive single-stock volatility more than index moves. Watch stock news before the index.

    December
