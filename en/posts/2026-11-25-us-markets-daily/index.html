US Markets Daily Report · November 25

One day was enough to undo Monday. Semiconductors rolled over again, leaving the S&P 500 down 0.50% at 7,201.09 and the Nasdaq down 1.48% at 23,733.54. Nvidia fell 5.86%, handing back about half of its 31% gain, Micron lost 11.0% and AMD 8.9%, and the SOX dropped 7.88% with all thirty members lower. Anthropic closed at $81.91, below its $83 IPO price. The Dow rose 0.85% and the Russell 2000 added 0.7%. Talk of a new tariff round and a December rate hike weighed on sentiment, while the 10-year yield eased to 4.901%.

The S&P 500 fell 0.50%, or 36.08 points, to 7,201.09. That is eight losses in nine sessions. The gap to the October 6 record close of 7,818.93 widened back to 7.9%. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.48%, or 356.02 points, to 23,733.54, and the Nasdaq 100 lost 1.91% to 26,222.01. The Dow, by contrast, rose 0.85%, or 423.39 points, to 50,018.70 for a third straight gain, and the Russell 2000 added 0.7% to close near 2,689. Three indexes, three different days.

The centre of gravity was semiconductors. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 7.88%, or 755.55 points, to 8,832.50, with all thirty members lower. Monday's 14.03% gain was more than half erased in a session. The index sits 39.7% below its June 22 record of 14,655.29. Texas Instruments lost 11.25%, Micron 11.01%, AMD 8.86%, Intel 8.15% and Applied Materials 6.66%. The iShares Semiconductor ETF and the Roundhill Memory ETF each dropped 9%.

Two things drove it. First, Monday's rally was confirmed as what it was: not an earnings or order event but the political return of Jensen Huang. Nvidia fell 5.86% to $155.38, giving back half of Monday's 31.48% surge, and its market value slipped back to roughly $3.75 trillion. Second, tariffs. Reports spread during the session that the administration would soon unveil Section 301 duties on excess industrial capacity in 16 trading partners, stacking on top of existing levies. That reads as a cost.

Rates added to the weight. Markets price a 95–96% probability of a 25bp hike at the December 8–9 meeting, with 5% odds of 50bp, after New York Fed President John Williams said on the 23rd that there is still work to do.

Inside the index, money was moving rather than leaving. Of the eleven S&P sectors, technology fell nearly 3% and health care rose 3.01%. Consumer staples added 1.63%, real estate 1.24%, energy 0.94%, financials 0.82% and communication services 0.75%. Amgen, Nike and Salesforce led the Dow. November consumer confidence came in at 103.3, up 11.4 points from October's 91.9 and far above the 92.2 expected — the highest since January 2025, helped by the end of midterm uncertainty. Improving consumer data alongside rising hike odds is an unusual combination.

Bonds and currencies were quiet. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.901%, down 1.8bp, the lowest since November 9. The 2-year held near 4.69% and the 30-year stayed around 5.31%. The dollar index slipped 0.45% to 103.85. Gold settled 2.45% lower at $3,559.90 an ounce, a third below its January 29 record of $5,318.40 and 1.4% above the November 20 low of $3,512.30. Oil went the other way as vessels were struck near the Strait of Hormuz: WTI settled 2.35% higher at $82.05 and Brent 2.27% higher at $91.40. Bitcoin fell 2.4% to $59,765.

Seoul priced in Monday's Wall Street rally in full. The Kospi rose 6.32%, or 297.49 points, to 5,006.03, reclaiming 5,000 after three sessions, and the Kosdaq gained 4.79% to 665.88. Foreigners turned net buyers for the first time in eight sessions with 725.5 billion won, and institutions added 869.2 billion won. Samsung Electronics, LG Display, Hanwha Life and Hanon Systems topped the foreign buying list. Buy-side sidecars triggered in both markets. The won closed at 1,342.4, up 3.5 against the dollar. Overnight, though, Wall Street's chips broke down again, which is a headwind for Seoul on the 25th.

After the bell the tape belonged to hardware. Dell reported $54.94 billion of revenue and $9.45 of adjusted EPS, blowing past estimates and raising full-year guidance for the third time this year. HP also beat with $16.0 billion of revenue and rose 14% after hours. HPE, which raised its own outlook, fell 16% on a margin warning. Three AI hardware names, three different verdicts; Dell's numbers and what they mean are the subject of this issue's spotlight.

NVDA · Nvidia

$155.38 (-5.86%). Half of Monday's 31.48% gain gone in a session. The stock opened about 3% lower, extended the loss past 7% and recovered some of it into the close. Jensen Huang apologised to employees at his first all-hands since returning, acknowledging a breach of the company's code of conduct; the New York Times reported he called it the hardest meeting he had ever held. Washington kept up the pressure. Senate Banking Committee chairman Tim Scott said Huang will testify at a December 3 public hearing on export controls, and Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats will investigate the administration and Nvidia executives once they take Congress next year. The board's independent committee review is ongoing. Strategy Asset Managers CEO Tom Hulick said Huang may still be the right person to run the company "for now," but "a lot of things to worry about" on governance remain. The stock is still more than 30% below its October 6 record.

MSFT · Microsoft

$496.13 (+0.74%). One of only two M7 names to rise on a day when chips broke. It traded above $500 at one point. There was no company news; money simply moved toward platforms that own their models and cloud. UBS Global Wealth Management's Mark Haefele wrote that the volatility in global tech "does not signal that the structural AI investment story has broken," while expecting "greater differentiation between established platforms with visible monetization and strong balance sheets, and companies dependent on debt, scarce capacity, or continued speculative growth." Tuesday was a preview of that differentiation.

AAPL · Apple

$354.07 (+1.43%). The stock that fell least last week rose the most in the M7 on Tuesday. Its distance from the AI capex debate explains both. The memory cost problem has not gone away: Micron confirmed rising DRAM contract prices in its late-September report, and the squeeze on device makers will show up in fourth-quarter numbers.

GOOGL · Alphabet

$279.05 (-1.08%). It lost the $280 line as Anthropic's slide below its IPO price marked down Alphabet's stake. Alphabet was the largest investor at Anthropic's listing, putting in $10 billion. There is still no direct answer to the report that Google cut 2027 TPU 8i orders. Broadcom's December 9 results will face the same question.

META · Meta Platforms

$589.82 (-1.30%). Monday's recovery above $600 lasted a day. The company spends more than any peer on its own models and data centres, which makes it the most sensitive to a tech pullback. Broadcom begins shipping Meta's inference chip (MTIA) in volume this quarter, which adds to the capex bill.

AMZN · Amazon

$252.29 (-1.21%). It gave back a slice of Monday's gain. The mark on its Anthropic stake moved the wrong way while the AWS capex burden stayed. Money rotated into staples and health care, neither of which describes Amazon.

TSLA · Tesla

$384.68 (-0.20%). Effectively flat, digesting Monday's 11.46% jump on the FSD v15 rollout. JPMorgan argued that a merger with SpaceX could let the S&P Dow Jones indices committee waive some eligibility rules on precedent, with passive demand potentially reaching 10% of SpaceX's float. SpaceX itself fell 6%, among the worst large-cap tech names. No merger agreement has been announced.

AVGO · Broadcom

$301.08 (-3.98%). Barely holding $300. A 4% decline on a day the SOX lost 7.9% is relative resilience, but buyers are not stepping up before the December 9 report. The Google TPU order report and the $42 billion Anthropic exposure still await answers. We covered the detail in the November 21 spotlight.

AMD · Advanced Micro Devices

$413.27 (-8.86%). The $450 line broke, erasing Monday's 6.85% gain entirely. With all thirty SOX members lower, this was sector beta rather than company news. Still, a slide back to the low $400s after repeated swings since October's short report suggests the positioning is not yet light.

MU · Micron

$629.79 (-11.01%). Nearly 60% of Monday's 19.04% surge returned. BNP Paribas Exane cut its rating on Micron, citing demand risk into 2028. SK hynix's ADR and SanDisk fell 8–12%. The stock is 48% below its June 25 record close of $1,213.56. The test is December 17, when fiscal first-quarter results must confirm the $61.5 billion guidance. We covered the detail in the November 24 spotlight.

TSM · TSMC

$349.43 (-3.90%). It dipped below $340 intraday before recovering part of the loss; Taipei fell in sympathy. No news pointed to weaker foundry demand — this was risk reduction, not an order revision.

INTC · Intel

$54.80 (-8.15%). Among the worst large-cap semis. The company's October guidance for a roughly 12% decline in the 2026 PC market still hangs over it, and Gartner put third-quarter global PC shipments down 22.4%.

TXN · Texas Instruments / ADI · Analog Devices

Texas Instruments fell 11.25%, the worst in the SOX. Analog Devices dropped 18% despite beating: adjusted EPS of $4.06 against $3.90 expected, revenue of $4.37 billion against $4.33 billion. The guidance was the problem. Analog chips track industrial and automotive demand, and the market read the outlook as evidence that a recovery there is slipping.

MRVL · Marvell / ARM · Arm

Marvell fell 9.10% and Arm 8.55%. Custom silicon and IP design had the biggest Monday gains, so they gave back the most. The expectation that Google is expanding MediaTek-designed volume still stands, but the timing is unconfirmed.

CRWV · CoreWeave / IREN · Iren / NBIS · Nebius

CoreWeave fell 11.84%, Iren 11.54% and Nebius 10.74% after Monday's 20%-plus gains. According to S3 Partners, neocloud short interest is about $11 billion, with CoreWeave alone at $3.6 billion, or 19.6% of its float. S3's Sam Pierson said shorts "are holding, not covering" — CoreWeave shares shorted slipped less than 1% during Monday's rally. CoreWeave remains more than 69% below its recent high.

ANT · Anthropic

$81.91 (-6.54%). It traded as low as $80.01 and closed below its $83 IPO price four sessions after listing. The over-allotment option has been exercised, so underwriters have no cushion left. A filing late Monday showed CEO Dario Amodei sold more than 3.37 million shares, worth over $280 million, the day after the debut. Wedbush initiated coverage at Outperform on enterprise AI demand. The stock did not care.

DELL · Dell / HPE · Hewlett Packard Enterprise / HPQ · HP

Dell closed at $295.28 (-11.83%) ahead of its report, giving back more than half of Monday's 20.46% gain. After the bell it reported $54.94 billion of revenue and $9.45 of adjusted EPS and raised its full-year revenue outlook to $202 billion; the shares ran up as much as 6% and then gave most of it back. HPE beat with $14.55 billion of revenue (up 50%) and $1.43 of EPS and lifted fiscal 2027 growth guidance to 27–35%, but warned that Cloud & AI margins fall to about 14%, and the stock fell 16%. HP reported $16.0 billion of revenue (up 9.3%) and $0.83 of EPS, with AI PCs at nearly 60% of shipments; operating margin fell to 5.3% from 6.6% and management called its fiscal 2027 outlook "prudent." The shares rose 14% after hours.

UNH · UnitedHealth / DIS · Walt Disney

UnitedHealth rose 1.73% to $341.88 and Disney 1.89% to $122.54 as money left technology for health care and communication services. In pharma, Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $41 billion, or $175 a share in cash — a 46% premium to the last close before the talks surfaced — the largest pharmaceutical deal since Pfizer bought Seagen for $43 billion in 2023. Insmed closed up 14.1% at $170.15 and Sanofi rose 10.9% in Paris.

What to check next

  1. 01
    Tariffs

    The 16-economy overcapacity report — Korea is capped at 15%

    The U.S. Trade Representative is expected to publish the results of its Section 301 excess-capacity investigation this week, possibly next. The 16 economies are China, the EU, India, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, Vietnam, Taiwan, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Switzerland, Norway, Singapore, Bangladesh and Cambodia. Nothing had been released by Tuesday's close. The new duties would stack on top of the administration's second-term tariffs, with goods already covered by Section 232 — steel, aluminium, autos — likely excluded. The White House says it will honour the ceilings written into existing trade deals: 15% for the EU, Japan and South Korea, 19% for Malaysia. The open question is whether the effective Korean ceiling stays at 15% once a new duty is layered over the 12.5% forced-labour tariff imposed in July. For China, the 20% cap holds only if the truce expiring January 10 is extended.

    November 25–26 (ET) · USTR announcement
  2. 02
    Rates

    October PCE and third-quarter GDP on the 25th — the last check before December

    October PCE inflation lands at 22:30 KST on the 25th, with core expected up 0.3% on the month. Revised third-quarter GDP is due the same day and is expected to confirm 3.4% growth. Markets price a 95–96% chance of a 25bp hike at the December 8–9 FOMC, with 5% odds of 50bp. New York Fed President John Williams said on the 23rd that there is "still work to do" and sees inflation finishing 2026 near 3.5%. A core print above 0.3% reopens the 50bp debate.

    November 25, 22:30 KST · December 8–9 FOMC
  3. 03
    Margins

    AI servers up, fourth-quarter profit down — the question Dell left behind

    Dell reported $54.94 billion of revenue and $9.45 of EPS, then guided the fourth quarter to $56.3 billion of revenue and $8.40 of EPS — revenue up 2.5%, earnings per share down by more than a dollar. HPE, reporting the same day, raised its full-year outlook sharply and still fell 16% on a warning that first-quarter margins would compress. AI server vendors are reported to be asking for 25–45% price increases on existing contracts. The suspicion that memory costs are inflating revenue while pinching profit has followed the group since Barclays downgraded it on October 14.

    Late February 2027 · Dell's fourth quarter
  4. 04
    Regulation

    December 3 hearing — the reinstatement is done, the investigation is not

    Jensen Huang apologised to employees at his first all-hands since returning, acknowledging he had violated the company's code of conduct. Washington has only tightened its grip. Senate Banking Committee chairman Tim Scott said Huang will appear at a December 3 public hearing on export controls. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats will investigate the administration and Nvidia executives once they take Congress next year, and Senator Elizabeth Warren has demanded the White House disclose who was involved in the reinstatement. The board's independent committee review continues.

    December 3 · Senate Banking hearing
  5. 05
    Positioning

    The VIX is back at 26.4, but it traded at 32.38

    The volatility index opened at 26.52, ran to 32.38 and closed at 26.43. Headline moves were small; the intraday range was not. The S&P 500 remains below its 200-day moving average. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky recommends using rallies above 7,300 to cut exposure. Thursday the 26th is closed for Thanksgiving and Friday the 27th is a 13:00 close, so thinner volume can amplify moves on the same news.

    November 26–27 · holiday session
  6. 06
    Korea

    The Kospi took back 5,000 in a single day

    The Kospi rose 6.32% to 5,006.03, reclaiming the 5,000 line after three sessions. The Kosdaq gained 4.79% to 665.88. Foreigners turned net buyers for the first time in eight sessions, adding 725.5 billion won, and institutions bought 869.2 billion won. Samsung Electronics, LG Display, Hanwha Life and Hanon Systems led the foreign buying list. Buy-side sidecars triggered in both markets. The won closed 3.5 higher at 1,342.4 per dollar. Wall Street's second semiconductor sell-off overnight is a headwind for Seoul on the 25th.
