China AI Daily Report · November 27

A State Council executive meeting at midday, with its reference to a 'stabilisation fund' role for Central Huijin, turned the whole A-share market around. Shanghai rose 3.73% to 3,606.49 and reclaimed 3,600; ChiNext jumped 11.61% and the STAR 50 15.07%, the largest ChiNext day since 8 October 2024 and the STAR 50's third-best on record. Turnover rose CNY 682bn to CNY 1.9998tn, with 5,382 stocks up against 181 down. All eleven of our names rose: Zhongji Innolight +19.98%, limit-ups for Eoptolink, Piotech, VeriSilicon, Shengyi and GigaDevice, Cambricon +18.68%, Hygon +18.05%, Montage +15.69%, SMIC +15.38% and CXMT +12.97%. Zhongji Innolight led the market on both turnover (CNY 33.397bn) and main-fund inflow (CNY 3.769bn).

The Shanghai and Shenzhen markets alone traded CNY 1.9998tn; including Beijing the total was CNY 2.0173tn (+CNY 691.3bn). Shanghai accounted for CNY 929.8bn (previous day CNY 613.3bn) and Shenzhen CNY 1.0700tn (previous day CNY 704.0bn). Nine stocks traded more than CNY 10bn: Zhongji Innolight (CNY 33.397bn), Eoptolink (CNY 26.924bn), CXMT (CNY 18.564bn) and Cambricon (CNY 18.500bn) took the top four, CATL was fifth at CNY 15.230bn and SMIC seventh at CNY 12.307bn.

The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,606.49 (+3.73%), reclaiming 3,600 for the first time in weeks. It opened at 3,482.38, traded as low as 3,467.41 and as high as 3,610.72, and spent the morning drifting near 3,480 before turning vertical just after midday. The Shenzhen Component rose 7.24% to 10,814.02, ChiNext 11.61% to 2,443.96, the STAR 50 15.07% to 1,346.25, the STAR Composite 14.21% to 1,489.77 and the BSE 50 13.20% to 851.79. The CSI 300 added 4.17% to 4,063.06 and the SSE 50 2.94% to 2,834.68. ChiNext's gain was its largest since October 8, 2024; the STAR 50's was its third-largest ever, behind September 30 and October 8, 2024.

Breadth matched the headline. Gainers outnumbered decliners 5,382 to 181, with 96% of stocks higher, 426 limit-ups and one limit-down. Turnover across Shanghai and Shenzhen was CNY 1.9998tn, up CNY 682.4bn from the previous session. Shanghai traded CNY 929.8bn (from CNY 613.3bn) and Shenzhen CNY 1.0700tn (from CNY 704.0bn); including Beijing the total reached CNY 2.0173tn. Turnover that had fallen for three straight sessions doubled in a day.

The trigger came at midday from the State Council. An executive meeting on capital-market investment and financing reform said it would keep improving the market's stabilisation mechanism, give fuller play to the "national team" and further optimise Central Huijin's quasi-stabilisation-fund function to ensure ample liquidity. It also called for more dividends and buybacks, better policies to channel insurers, enterprise annuities and the social-security fund into equities, stronger defences against imported risk, and full-chain supervision. It was the second time in a month that the cabinet had promised to improve the stabilisation mechanism, after November 6, and the first time it named the stabilisation-fund role. The line that moved prices was the promise to "devise incremental policies" as well as implement existing ones. Neither the size nor the mechanism has been disclosed; the Central Economic Work Conference in December is the next checkpoint.

Semiconductors led the afternoon. The sector rose 14.97% on CNY 280.4bn of turnover; electronic chemicals added 14.53%, communications equipment 11.46%, software 11.42%, IT services 11.36%, components 11.22%, automation equipment 11.19% and consumer electronics 10.21%. Eighty-nine sectors rose and only banks fell (-1.07%). High-dividend names such as China Shenhua, Sinopec and China Mobile gave back morning gains while growth stocks took the money. Main funds bought CNY 23.824bn of semiconductors and sold banks, agricultural processing and white goods. By theme, chip concepts drew CNY 63.47bn, data centres CNY 42.85bn, Huawei-related names CNY 41.71bn and semiconductor equipment CNY 3.2bn. At the stock level Zhongji Innolight (+CNY 3.769bn), Eoptolink (+CNY 2.921bn), CXMT (+CNY 2.475bn), Shengyi (+CNY 1.551bn) and GigaDevice (+CNY 1.254bn) led.

Within chips, the equipment chain had news of its own. Jingce Electronics disclosed after the close on November 26 that a subsidiary had signed a CNY 199.8m contract to supply front-end metrology tools. On November 27, reports said new orders at AMEC, ACM Research and peers had hit records in the first nine months, and that domestic tools had taken more than half of purchases on the third-phase line of a leading memory maker for the first time. ACM's backlog was CNY 17.073bn at end-September, up 88.20% year on year. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology approved the industry's first dedicated quality-management standard for semiconductor equipment and components. SEMI expects global 300mm front-end equipment spending of USD 149bn in 2026, up 31%. National Bureau of Statistics data for January-October showed electronics profits up 1.0x, contributing 9.7 percentage points to industrial profit growth. Orders, standards and profits were confirmed by numbers on the same day.

Policy and export controls pointed the same way. On November 26 Guangdong published its 15th five-year plan for compute, targeting 1,000 EFlops (FP16) of intelligent computing by 2028, encouraging compute centres to prioritise domestic chips and to build out the CANN ecosystem and Ascend operators. The same day the Financial Times reported that Nvidia had told Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, following the US interim final rule published on November 19. If the route through Southeast Asian data centres is closed, the argument goes, demand shifts back to domestic compute and domestic chips and tools. Domestic AI chips, equipment, optical modules and PCBs all rose together.

Asia was already rebounding. New York was closed overnight for Thanksgiving and shut early at 1pm on November 27, leaving little external direction. Even so the Kospi rose 4.34% to 4,915.68, reclaiming 4,900 for the first time in three sessions, and the Kosdaq jumped 8.13% to 708.99 with a buy-side sidecar triggered at 10:22am. Foreigners bought KRW 1.13tn of Kospi shares, Samsung Electronics rose 4.55% and SK Hynix 5.41%. The Nikkei added 2.11% to 58,711.11 and Kioxia 8.86%. Hong Kong followed, with the Hang Seng +2.11%, the HSCEI +2.01% and the Hang Seng Tech index +5.45%. The won closed at 1,337.9 per dollar, up 4.8.

Every one of our eleven names rose. Five hit their caps: Eoptolink, Piotech and VeriSilicon at +20%, Shengyi and GigaDevice at +10%. Zhongji Innolight added 19.98%, Cambricon 18.68%, Hygon 18.05%, Montage 15.69%, SMIC 15.38% and CXMT 12.97%. Together the eleven traded CNY 148.1bn, 7.4% of the whole market. Zhongji Innolight took both the turnover crown (CNY 33.397bn) and the largest main-fund inflow (CNY 3.769bn); CXMT turned over 11.75% of its float, the most in the group. At the other end Hygon (1.85%) and Cambricon (2.85%) traded thinly relative to their size and share price.

What to watch

  1. 01
    Policy

    Beijing puts the 'stabilisation fund' back on the table

    At midday on November 27 a State Council executive meeting chaired by Premier Li Qiang took up capital-market reform and said it would keep improving the market's stabilisation mechanism, give full play to the 'national team', and further optimise Central Huijin's quasi-stabilisation-fund function to ensure ample liquidity. It also called for more dividends and buybacks, better support for insurers, enterprise annuities and the social-security fund to enter the market, and defences against imported risk. It was the second such reference in a month, after November 6. What moved prices was the promise to 'devise incremental policies' alongside existing ones. No size or mechanism has been published; the Central Economic Work Conference in December is the next checkpoint.

    December Central Economic Work Conference (around 11th)
  2. 02
    Trade

    Section 301 comments close January 5, the truce expires January 10

    On November 25 the US Trade Representative finalised determinations against 14 economies over structural excess capacity and proposed additional tariffs of up to 20% on Chinese goods, assuming last October's Kuala Lumpur arrangement holds; the rate would rise to 40% if the truce collapses. Semiconductors are among the sectors named. Written comments are due January 5, 2027, hearings begin January 25, and the truce expires January 10. Those three dates survive even on a day like this.

    January 5, 10 and 25, 2027
  3. 03
    Export controls

    Closing the Southeast Asian detour stopped Nvidia's servers

    The US Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on November 16, effective on publication on November 19, requiring licences for certain advanced AI chips shipped to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, and extending controls to data-centre compute leasing. Existing data centres have 90 days to obtain licences. On November 26 the Financial Times reported that Nvidia had told Dell, Supermicro and other partners to stop shipping servers to the four Southeast Asian countries; Singapore's deputy prime minister Gan Kim Yong said on the 27th that the city-state had not confirmed the halt. The shift is a tailwind for domestic chips and tools and a sourcing risk for optical-module and server makers with Southeast Asian plants.

    90-day grace period ends mid-February 2027
  4. 04
    Equipment

    Domestic tool share above 50% is the basis for this rally

    Reports on November 27 said new orders at AMEC, ACM Research and other domestic equipment makers hit records in the first nine months, and that domestic tools accounted for more than half of purchases on the third phase line of a leading memory maker for the first time. Jingce Electronics disclosed a CNY 199.8m front-end metrology order on the evening of November 26; ACM's order backlog stood at CNY 17.073bn at end-September (+88.20%). The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology approved the industry's first dedicated quality-management standard for semiconductor equipment and components, and SEMI expects global 300mm front-end equipment spending of USD 149bn in 2026, up 31%. Statistics issued the same day showed electronics profits up 1.0x in January-October, contributing 9.7 percentage points to industrial profit growth.

    December orders, January results
  5. 05
    Macro

    November PMI on the 30th, the Fed on December 8-9

    November manufacturing PMI is due at 09:30 Beijing time on November 30 (10:30 KST). October's reading was 49.3 after 50.1 in September, so a return above 50 would decide whether the policy trade lasts. November CPI and PPI follow on December 9, and industrial output, retail sales and fixed investment on December 15. The Fed meets on December 8-9 with a December hike fully priced. Margin financing across the two exchanges stood at CNY 2.11tn on November 26; the leverage behind a CNY 2tn day can amplify any pullback.

    November 30 · December 8-9
  6. 06
    Flows

    ChiNext's 11% day sets up next week's test

    ChiNext's 11.61% was its biggest one-day gain since October 8, 2024, and the STAR 50's 15.07% was the third-largest on record after September 30 and October 8, 2024. Breadth was wide: 5,382 gainers against 181 decliners and 426 limit-ups. The speed is the issue. A market whose turnover had fallen for three sessions and whose chips were being sold only a day earlier suddenly traded CNY 2tn. If policy expectations do not turn into execution, the reversal can be as fast. Watch turnover, margin balances and ETF creations in the first week of December.

    First week of December
