US Markets Daily Report · November 26

Wall Street gave back almost all of an early slide. The S&P 500 was down 3.2% at its low, the Nasdaq 4.6% and almost 1,100 points on the Dow, and the session ended with the S&P down 0.25%, the Nasdaq down 0.62% and the Dow up 0.33%; the SOX fell nearly 8% intraday and closed down 0.65%. October PCE printed at 3.8%, a three-year high, third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3%, and Washington proposed tariffs of up to 20-40% on 14 trading partners. Nvidia fell 3.91%, while Meta rose 3.40%.

The S&P 500 closed down 0.25%, or 18.31 points, at 7,182.78, after trading 3.2% lower. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62%, or 147.74 points, to 23,585.80, and the Dow rose 0.33%, or 164.25 points, to 50,182.95 after being down more than 1,100 points, or 2.2%. The Nasdaq recovered from down 4.6%. The Russell 2000 added 0.71% to 2,704.05, beating the large-cap indexes. The S&P 500 sits 8.14% below its October 6 record of 7,818.93 and is down roughly 5% for November.

The weight was still in AI, but the direction flipped. The PHLX Semiconductor Index opened at 8,302.93, traded as low as 8,129.23 and closed at 8,774.86, down 0.65%, or 57.45 points — a nearly 8% intraday loss reduced to a fraction. With the open 6% below the previous close, the whole session was a buying exercise. Nvidia fell 3.91%, Micron 4.53%, Alphabet 2.13% and Amazon 3.12%. Oracle fell more than 5% toward its lowest close since May 2023, and CoreWeave dropped more than 12% before finishing flat at $42.54. Anthropic rose 1.08% to $82.79, its first gain since breaking its IPO price, and Meta added 3.40%.

The macro news went the wrong way. October PCE rose 0.3% on the month and 3.8% from a year earlier — in line, but up from 3.7% in September and the highest since May 2023. Core PCE came in at 3.3% against a 3.2% forecast. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3% from 3.4%, with real final sales to private domestic purchasers up 5.8% against 4.9%. Strong growth without cooling inflation pushed the odds of a 25bp hike at the December 8-9 meeting to 95-97%. Treasuries rallied anyway: the 10-year fell to 4.848%, down more than 5bp, and the 2-year dropped 8bp to 4.588%. December is priced; the market is now arguing about what follows.

Tariffs were softer than the headline. The USTR published 14 excess-capacity determinations and proposed duties of up to 20-40%, but most are calculated net of MFN and existing Section 301 and 232 duties, so the incremental cost is small. China is the exception, with 20% on top of first-term duties of about 25% and 40% held in reserve. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer described the effect as "more like a top-up". Just as important, these duties replace the IEEPA tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February — a legal rebuild of an existing burden rather than a new one. Comments are due January 5 and hearings begin January 25.

The rebound was about positioning. Volume ran below average into the holiday, and a blog post from Michael Burry saying his portfolio was "temporarily almost entirely long" was credited with lifting the afternoon. Barclays' Venu Krishna called it "panic" and said the market was "expecting some sort of a macro shock". Beneath the index, materials (about 2%), real estate, energy and industrials rose while technology lagged, and the Dow was lifted by IBM, Salesforce and Sherwin-Williams. The VIX touched 34.95 intraday and settled at 28.08, up 6.5% from 26.36 but well off the morning's fear.

Korea took Tuesday's Wall Street loss in full. The Kospi fell 6.16%, or 308.52 points, to 4,697.51, breaking 4,700, and a sell-side sidecar was triggered early. The Kosdaq lost 2.29% to 650.64. Foreigners sold 1.95 trillion won a day after turning buyers. The won closed at 1,343.2 per dollar, up 0.8, after touching 1,349 in the morning. Because New York recovered most of its intraday loss, the Korean market has room to retrace part of that fall on the 26th. The U.S. is closed today and closes early tomorrow, so the next full session opens at 00:30 KST on the 28th.

NVDA · Nvidia

$149.30 (-3.91%). It closed at $149.30 from $155.38, having traded down to $147.37 in premarket. That is another 10% or so given back over three sessions from Monday's 31.48% jump. Wednesday's move was sector flow rather than company news, but the December 3 Senate Banking hearing on export controls and the board's independent review are still ahead. The stock is well below both its 50-day ($204.95) and 200-day ($203.07) averages, so any rebound runs into resistance before long.

MSFT · Microsoft

$489.93 (-1.25%). It gave back Tuesday's gain and slipped back below $490. The cloud-and-own-models combination that made it a defensive name on Tuesday did not help on Wednesday, though its loss was smaller than Oracle's 5% and Amazon's 3.12%. If Broadcom's December 9 report reopens the Google TPU debate, Microsoft's smaller custom-silicon exposure becomes a relative selling point to test.

AAPL · Apple

$355.58 (+0.43%). The only M7 name to close higher. Its distance from the AI capex debate showed up again on a day like this. It trades above both its 50-day ($342.58) and 200-day ($303.99) averages. Memory costs remain the offset, and they will show up in set-makers' fourth-quarter margins.

GOOGL · Alphabet

$273.10 (-2.13%). The Anthropic stake and the TPU order-cut story landed together, and as one of the largest AI infrastructure spenders it sat in the middle of the selloff. Anthropic rebounded and Alphabet did not. Broadcom's December 9 results and the 2027 TPU 8i order plan are the next checkpoints.

META · Meta Platforms

$609.89 (+3.40%). The standout move of the session. It opened at $575.34, down 2.5%, traded as low as $567.75 (-3.75%), and then reversed more than 7% to close at $610. It added 5.1 points to the S&P 500, the largest single-stock contribution. Reports that Amazon and Meta are negotiating to restore the Muse AI shopping agent, a $4.5 billion GPU-backed data centre loan, and holiday ad expectations all helped. JPMorgan cut its target to $775 from $940 the same day. The full picture is in this issue's spotlight.

AMZN · Amazon

$244.43 (-3.12%). The worst of the three clouds, taking 9.0 points off the S&P 500. It is on the other side of the Muse talks, but the market priced the risk to its advertising and commerce relationship first. AWS capex and the Anthropic stake add to the pressure.

TSLA · Tesla

$380.14 (-1.18%). Back to $380 as it continues to digest the self-driving software news that lifted it more than 11% on Tuesday. The SpaceX merger remains unannounced and is not yet a tradable catalyst.

AVGO · Broadcom

$300.86 (-0.07%). Flat, right on $300. With fourth-quarter results due December 9 it has not picked a direction. The Google TPU order cut, Anthropic exposure and the start of MTIA inference-chip shipments all get resolved at once. See the November 21 spotlight for the detail.

AMD · Advanced Micro Devices

Down 1.56%. It gave back part of Tuesday's 6.85% gain. On a day when the SOX fell nearly 8% intraday and closed down 0.65%, a 1.56% loss was relatively strong. The post-October short-interest report flow that has produced repeated swings has not lightened.

MU · Micron

$601.25 (-4.53%). It held $600 by a thread, giving back another quarter of Tuesday's 19% jump. BNP Paribas Exane's downgrade on 2028 demand still stands, and the question is how the reported 25-45% contract price increase requests are split between cost and margin. December 17 results and the $61.5 billion guide are the next test.

TSM · TSMC

$342.34 (-1.96%). Taipei fell overnight too. There was no news of AI chip orders actually falling; this was risk reduction rather than foundry demand.

INTC · Intel

$54.49 (-0.57%). One of the smaller declines among large-cap chips. The 12% PC market decline expected for 2026 and Gartner's 22.4% drop in third-quarter PC shipments remain a drag, but Intel sat outside Wednesday's AI flow.

ORCL · Oracle

Down more than 5%. It headed for its lowest close since May 24, 2023, and has fallen in nine of the past ten sessions. As a core AI infrastructure supplier it was among the first to be sold. Its lagging cloud share and debt-funded data centre expansion are both cited. It is the same story as CoreWeave's widening credit spreads.

CRWV · CoreWeave / NBIS · Nebius

CoreWeave traded as low as $37.40 (-12%) and closed flat at $42.54. Its five-year CDS widened the most on record and its 9% 2031 bonds traded at 18% yields, while the equity recovered everything. The $4.5 billion Meta-backed loan sale next week will show real demand for AI infrastructure debt. Nebius also fell nearly 7% before finishing higher.

ANT · Anthropic

$82.79 (+1.08%). A modest rebound the day after breaking its $83 IPO price, having been up more than 4% intraday. Wedbush initiated at outperform on enterprise AI demand, but the stock stayed below the offer price. Alphabet's mark-to-market on its largest stake keeps swinging with it.

AMBA · Ambarella

Down 19%. Earnings and current-quarter guidance both missed. The market read it as edge-AI demand failing to recover, and it fell alone on a day when the SOX reversed. A case of single-name results beating sector flow.

Materials and defensives — IBM, Salesforce, Sherwin-Williams

The gains came from outside AI. Of the 11 S&P 500 sectors, materials, real estate, energy, industrials, communication services and financials rose, while technology, consumer discretionary and health care fell. The Dow was lifted by IBM, Salesforce and Sherwin-Williams. With crude rebounding, WTI settled at $83.57 a barrel, up 1.85%. Money leaving chips moved into materials and real estate for a day — narrow, but a real rotation.

What to check next

  1. 01
    Tariffs

    Proposed duties on 14 economies — Korea capped at 15%, with a 25% card held back

    The U.S. Trade Representative published the results of its Section 301 excess-capacity investigation on the 25th and proposed new duties on 14 economies: China, the EU, Korea, Japan, Taiwan, India, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, Cambodia, Bangladesh and Switzerland. Mexico and Norway, both under investigation, were left out. Headline rates run 20-40%, but most are calculated net of MFN rates and existing Section 301 and 232 duties, so the actual additional burden is small. Korea is proposed at up to 15% under its trade deal, and the 12.5% forced-labour duty imposed in July counts toward that ceiling rather than stacking on top. USTR also wrote in a path to 25% if consultations with Korea are unsatisfactory, and named autos, semiconductors and petrochemicals as the problem sectors. China gets 20% on top of first-term duties of about 25%, with 40% held if the truce fails. Because these duties replace the IEEPA tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February, the market read them as a legal rebuild of an existing burden rather than a new one. Comments are due January 5, with hearings on January 25.

    January 5 comments · January 25 hearings
  2. 02
    Rates

    October PCE at 3.8%, a three-year high — December is priced; January is the question

    The PCE price index rose 3.8% year over year in October, in line with expectations but up from a revised 3.7% in September and the highest since May 2023. Core PCE came in at 3.3% against a 3.2% forecast. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP was revised up 0.9 percentage point to 4.3%, with real final sales to private domestic purchasers up 5.8%. Strong growth with sticky inflation lifted the odds of a 25bp hike at the December 8-9 FOMC to 95-97%. Yet Treasuries rallied: the 10-year fell more than 5bp to 4.848% and the 2-year dropped 8bp to 4.588%. December is settled; what the market is repricing is the path after it.

    December 8-9 FOMC · January meeting
  3. 03
    Credit

    CoreWeave's CDS widened the most on record — the first real test for AI debt

    CoreWeave's five-year credit default swaps widened by the most ever in a single day, pricing a better-than-70% chance of default within five years. Its 9% bonds due 2031 traded at record-low prices to yield 18%. Yet the same company's $4.5 billion loan, secured by GPUs for a Meta-dedicated data centre in Dalton, Georgia, is due to be sold next week by Morgan Stanley and MUFG, and is expected to carry investment-grade ratings thanks to the Meta contract. Pricing is being discussed around 7-7.5%. Investors have registered interest matching the deal size. It is the clearest read yet on how much appetite exists for AI infrastructure debt.

    Next week · $4.5 billion loan sale
  4. 04
    Flows

    Closed Thursday, early close Friday — a thin tape produced a 7% reversal

    Half of Wednesday's rebound was positioning. The Dow erased a 1,100-point decline to finish higher and the Nasdaq recovered from down 4.6% to down 0.62%. Volume ran below average into the holiday, and a blog post by Michael Burry saying his portfolio was "temporarily almost entirely long" was credited with lifting the afternoon. The Nasdaq 100 briefly touched 20% below its record close, the threshold of a bear market, before recovering. Markets are closed on the 26th for Thanksgiving and close at 1 p.m. ET on the 27th. The next full session opens at 00:30 KST on the 28th.

    November 26 closed · November 27 early close
  5. 05
    Korea

    Kospi falls 6.16% — 4,700 gives way as foreigners sell 1.95 trillion won

    The Kospi closed down 308.52 points, or 6.16%, at 4,697.51, a day after reclaiming 5,000. A sell-side sidecar was triggered early in the session. The Kosdaq fell 2.29% to 650.64. Foreigners turned net sellers again, offloading 1.95 trillion won. The won closed at 1,343.2 per dollar, up 0.8 won, after touching 1,349 in the morning. The trigger was Tuesday's 7.88% fall in the SOX. Because New York recovered most of its intraday loss on Wednesday, the Korean market has room to retrace part of that drop on the 26th.

    November 25 Korea · November 26 open
  6. 06
    Earnings

    Broadcom on December 9, Micron on December 17 — the second read on AI capex

    The next large catalysts are Broadcom's fiscal fourth quarter on December 9, where the reported cut to Google's 2027 TPU 8i orders, Anthropic exposure and the start of MTIA inference-chip shipments all get tested, and Micron's fiscal first quarter on December 17, where the question is whether it delivers on its $61.5 billion guide. Dell reports in late February. The chips held up better than the index on Wednesday; Micron did not. Both are waiting on those three numbers.

    December 9 · December 17
