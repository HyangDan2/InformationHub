US Markets Spotlight · META — Record ads, negative free cash flow

Third-quarter revenue of $64.6 billion (+26%), average price per ad up 14% and 3.64 billion daily users — by the numbers, a record quarter. The same quarter produced negative free cash flow of $2.12 billion, the first in more than a decade, and operating margin fell from 41% to 20%. Capital spending for 2026 is guided to $135-145 billion, roughly double 2025. On the 25th the stock reversed from -3.7% to +3.4% on Amazon talks over its Muse shopping agent and a $4.5 billion Meta-backed data centre loan, while JPMorgan cut its target to $775 from $940.

Meta moved more than 7% within a single session on the 25th. It opened at $575.34, down 2.5%, traded as low as $567.75 (-3.75%), and closed at $609.89 (+3.40%). On a day the Nasdaq fell 0.62% and Nvidia 3.91%, it was one of two M7 names to finish higher and the largest single-stock contributor to the S&P 500. JPMorgan cut its target to $775 from $940 the same day. The catalysts and the ratings pointed in opposite directions.

The company in one sentence: it earns record cash from advertising and spends more than that on AI infrastructure. Third-quarter revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion while free cash flow came in at -$2.12 billion, the first negative quarter in more than a decade, and the stock sits 22% below its September 24 high of $777.59. The market's question is narrow. How much more advertising revenue can this spending buy?

The company, and where it sits in the chain

Meta runs two segments: the Family of Apps — Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger — and Reality Labs, which holds the glasses and VR hardware. Of 2025 revenue of $200.97 billion, advertising was $196.18 billion, or 97.6%. Reality Labs brought in $2.2 billion and lost $19.2 billion. Daily users across the family were 3.64 billion in the third quarter. Headcount was 66,775, down 15% from a year earlier after the May reduction of about 8,000.

In the value chain Meta is both the seller of advertising and one of the largest buyers of AI infrastructure. Almost all of the revenue comes from ads, and AI is what makes those ads perform: ranking and recommendation, creative generation, and automated targeting through Advantage+. Management's argument in the third quarter was exactly this — that the AI investment is already lifting advertising revenue in the core business.

The money goes out on the other side. Chips come from Nvidia and AMD; the in-house MTIA inference chip is built with Broadcom. Data centres include the largest campus the company has ever built, in Louisiana, plus capacity leased from CoreWeave in Dalton, Georgia. That compute is split between model training at Meta Superintelligence Labs and consumer products — Meta AI and Muse. In hardware, the AI glasses built with EssilorLuxottica are the only Reality Labs product with meaningful revenue.

Competition runs on two levels. In advertising Meta splits budgets with Google, Amazon, TikTok and Pinterest; Amazon is the one taking shopping-search ad budgets and Google the one defending search and YouTube. In AI it competes with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind on both models and consumer agents. What makes Muse notable is not model quality but that Meta was first to put an agent in front of 3.6 billion people with payments attached.

The quarter, and the numbers that matter

Third-quarter revenue was $64.60 billion, up 26% from $51.24 billion a year earlier. Advertising is nearly all of it: impressions rose 10% while average price per ad rose 14%, after 19% and 12% respectively in the first quarter. The axis of growth has flipped entirely from volume to price. Daily users rose 3% to 3.64 billion.

Costs are the problem. Total costs and expenses rose 68% to $51.61 billion, of which $9.89 billion was the charge for the August agreement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental health, a settlement of up to $18.59 billion. Excluding it, operating income would have been $22.88 billion, up 11%, at a 35% margin. As reported, operating income was $12.99 billion, down 37%, and the margin fell from 41% to 20%. EPS was $3.83, or $7.40 excluding the charge. The 24% tax rate, above the guided 15-17%, is also a consequence of the non-deductible charge.

Cash flow is where the change in character shows. Third-quarter operating cash flow was $32.79 billion — still enormous — but capital spending was $34.91 billion, more than that. Free cash flow was -$2.12 billion, against +$10.63 billion a year earlier, a $12.8 billion swing. Quarterly capex went from $19.84 billion to $31.08 billion to $34.91 billion in three quarters. Cash and marketable securities were $82.45 billion against $80.92 billion of long-term debt; a year earlier long-term debt was $58.74 billion.

Guidance is more aggressive still. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided to $73-76 billion, 2026 total expenses to $175-179 billion, and capital spending to $135-145 billion. That range started the year at $115-135 billion, was raised to $125-145 billion in the first quarter and narrowed to $130-145 billion in the second. It is double 2025's $72.2 billion. With $85.8 billion spent in the first three quarters, the fourth quarter alone must carry $49-59 billion. Reality Labs losses are guided to $18-19 billion for 2026 and around $15 billion in 2027.

Valuation is low by this company's standards. At a market cap of about $1.56 trillion against trailing EPS of $29.33, the stock trades at 20.6 times earnings and 18.6 times forward. Of 62 analysts, 55 rate it buy or better and the average target is $842.54, 38% above the price. Yet targets have been coming down: JPMorgan to $775 from $940, Morgan Stanley to $720 from $820. Cutting the target while keeping the buy rating is a fair summary of where this stock stands.

What happened overnight, and the competitive picture

The first catalyst on the 25th was Amazon. According to Fortune, Amazon and Meta have held several rounds of talks to let Muse search and check out on Amazon directly. The sticking points are how search results are displayed and how advertising revenue is shared. Amazon blocked Muse two months ago over user-agreement violations. Andy Jassy has said third-party agents need a "fair exchange of value". Any deal needs both CEOs to sign off.

The second was financing. Morgan Stanley and MUFG are due to sell a $4.5 billion loan for CoreWeave next week, secured by GPUs for the Dalton, Georgia data centre and expected to earn investment-grade ratings because of the Meta contract, at 7-7.5%. CoreWeave is a junk-rated company whose unsecured bonds trade at 18%; the Meta-backed loan prices at less than half that. It is evidence that Meta's credit creates real value, and at the same time a measure of how much of Meta's capacity sits on one neocloud's balance sheet.

The third was advertising. Channel checks into the holiday shopping week were positive, and the read on automated tools such as Advantage+ improving conversion kept improving. The third-quarter numbers support it: impressions up 10% while price per ad rose 14% means advertisers are paying more for the same inventory.

The reason the stock is still 22% below its September high is funding and spending. On October 26 Meta announced a $97.5 billion equity raise, with Nvidia taking $3 billion and AMD $1 billion. On November 3 it sold $15 billion of dollar bonds and €9 billion of euro bonds. A company that once funded investment from cash now uses equity and debt together. On top of that, the Delaware suit has frozen Class C issuance, and with it the $60 billion at-the-market programme — the cheapest leg of the funding plan, the one that raises money when the stock is high, is the one that is shut.

The competitive picture does not help. Google attacks with TPUs and Gemini, OpenAI and Anthropic through enterprise APIs, Amazon through commerce and cloud. Meta's way of winning has not changed: put AI on top of enormous distribution, raise the price of an ad, and pay for the buildout with that price. The third quarter showed the left side of that equation is still strong — and that the right side has, for the first time, grown larger.

Checkpoints and risks

  1. 01
    Litigation

    The Class C issuance is frozen — the first step of a $60 billion share sale has slipped

    In a November 23 filing Meta said it will not issue any Class C shares, by dividend or otherwise, while shareholder litigation in Delaware runs. The suits challenge the October 19 board-approved recapitalisation — one Class C share per Class A and B share, effectively a two-for-one split — and are scheduled for trial on April 19, 2027. The $60 billion at-the-market share programme, which cannot begin until Class C trades, is frozen with it. Meta had laid out a $97.5 billion fundraising plan on October 26-27 including $25 billion of common at $625 and $12.5 billion of convertible preferred, with Nvidia taking $3 billion and AMD $1 billion. If completed it would be the largest equity raise in the company's history.

    April 19, 2027 trial · ATM after that
  2. 02
    Earnings

    Late January — the first 2027 cost and capex numbers arrive with Q4

    Fourth-quarter revenue is guided to $73-76 billion. The real question is next year. On the third-quarter call the CFO said 2027 budgets and capacity plans were still being built and that quantitative guidance would come with the fourth-quarter report. JPMorgan models 2027 total expenses at $222 billion, up 25%, and capex at $255 billion, and cut its 2027-28 earnings estimates by 8-9%. A number above consensus widens the discount in the stock.

    Late January 2027 · Meta Q4 results
  3. 03
    Cash

    Negative $2.12 billion free cash flow — will one quarter be all of it?

    Third-quarter operating cash flow was $32.79 billion against $34.91 billion of capex, a $2.12 billion gap. A year earlier the quarter produced positive free cash flow of $10.63 billion, so $12.8 billion disappeared in a year. Cash and marketable securities were $82.45 billion at September 30 against $80.92 billion of long-term debt. Meta sold $15 billion of dollar bonds and €9 billion of euro bonds on November 3 and said it will not return to the unsecured market before the second quarter of next year. Committed future spending totals about $825 billion.

    Q4 results · 2027 capex guide
  4. 04
    Credit

    Funding through CoreWeave — a Meta contract creates the rating, and the contract is the risk

    Morgan Stanley and MUFG are due to sell a $4.5 billion loan next week, secured by GPUs for a Meta-dedicated data centre in Dalton, Georgia, expected to earn investment-grade ratings because of the Meta contract, at 7-7.5%. The same day CoreWeave's five-year CDS widened by the most on record, pricing a better-than-70% chance of default within five years, and its 9% 2031 bonds traded at 18% yields. Meta's GPU contracts with CoreWeave are estimated at about $14.2 billion in 2025 and up to $21 billion in 2026. Meta gets a lower cost of funds; the concentration of its capacity in one neocloud is the other side of the trade.

    Next week · $4.5 billion loan sale
  5. 05
    Regulation

    An $18.59 billion youth settlement, and pressure over data centres abroad

    The biggest reason third-quarter costs rose 68% is the August agreement with 52 state attorneys general over teen mental health, which provides for payments of up to $18.59 billion; $9.89 billion was charged in the third quarter and the rest lands in future periods. Several youth-related trials are scheduled in the U.S. Separately, President Trump said he would consider a ban or a "tremendous tax" on U.S. companies that build data centres abroad — part of why Meta is building its largest campus inside the United States.

    Charged each quarter · youth trial calendar
  6. 06
    Product

    Muse — 51% of AI-agent orders, with the Amazon talks as the next fork

    More than 10 million people in the U.S. and Canada had set up Meta's AI shopping agent by October 21, and management says more than 25% of active users shop with it at an average above $200. Forwarder Intelligence counted Meta behind 51% of orders placed by AI agents in the past week, with SpaceX's Grok second at 24%. Amazon blocked Muse two months ago citing user-agreement violations and is now negotiating over how search results are displayed and how advertising is handled. A deal would connect Muse to Amazon after Walmart. The offset: serving costs rise with every user.

    Amazon talks ongoing · Q4 results
