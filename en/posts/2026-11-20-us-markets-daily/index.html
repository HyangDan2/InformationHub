US Markets Daily Report · November 20

Anthropic rose 17% in its debut and the market went the other way. The S&P 500 fell 2.03% for a sixth straight loss, the Dow closed below 50,000 for the first time since June, and AMD lost 7.72% on a short-seller report. The VIX jumped 17.6% to 28.90 and futures now price a December hike at 95%.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,175.24 (-148.39, -2.03%), a sixth straight decline and its longest losing streak in two and a half years, and now sits 8.2% below its October 6 record. The Dow lost 1,102.71 points (-2.20%) to 49,095.46, its first close below 50,000 since June. The Nasdaq finished at 23,885.88 (-2.23%) and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) at 9,419.72 (-2.41%).

The intraday path was the story. All three indexes opened higher and the S&P 500 was up as much as 1.3% before turning, minutes after Anthropic's first trade at about 11:30 a.m. ET. The reversal was the largest since November 20, 2025. All eleven S&P 500 sectors fell, from energy (-0.65%) to consumer discretionary (-2.79%).

Three things made the day. Anthropic: priced at $83 on 980m shares to raise $81.3bn, it opened at $111.50 (+34%) and briefly carried a $2tn market value before closing at $97.17 (+17.1%). The problem was not Anthropic but everything around it: money that wanted the new AI stock had to come from somewhere, and the selling began the moment it started trading.

The Fed: the October minutes showed officials confident about a December hike while wanting more information, and named AI as "a material source of inflation risk". Governor Waller repeated that a half-point move may be needed. Futures price 25bp at 95%.

The data: initial jobless claims came in at 203,000 against 197,000 expected, and the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index fell to 6.3 from a 20.6 forecast. The Fed is still leaning toward hikes while the real economy cools.

Rates eased along the long end: the 10-year 5.046% (-2bp), the 30-year 5.402% (-1bp) and the 2-year 4.76% (-4bp). Crude went the other way, with WTI at $83.06 (+0.94%); Chevron (+0.4%) was the only Dow stock to close higher. The VIX jumped to 28.90 (+17.64%), its highest since March.

NVDA · Nvidia

$145.98 (-3.30%), a sixth straight loss and about 28% lower this month. The stock traded as high as $158.13 and as low as $145.71, closing almost exactly on its 52-week low. Results and guidance were settled last week; what caps the price now is the empty seat left by Jensen Huang's resignation, and there was no succession news on Thursday.

MSFT · Microsoft

$498.30 (-1.97%). It opened near $522, traded up to $525.64 and lost the $500 line in the afternoon. No company news: the Fed's warnings on AI capital spending and the portfolio reshuffle around Anthropic did the work.

AAPL · Apple

$349.21 (-0.92%), the smallest loss in the M7. Being outside the AI capex argument meant less to sell; it dipped to $346.61 and halved that loss by the close.

GOOGL · Alphabet

$272.66 (-3.46%). Alphabet holds 1.8bn Anthropic shares, which made it the most obvious beneficiary of the listing — and it fell harder than communication services (-2.67%). Some holders took the windfall, others worried about a tougher AI search fight. It traded as high as $294.40 early.

META · Meta Platforms

$596.22 (-3.27%), below $600 for the first time in weeks. This is a spending story, not an advertising one: names funding their own models and data centres are the first to be sold when AI sentiment wobbles.

AMZN · Amazon

$257.37 (-3.87%), the worst of the M7 despite owning 1.9bn Anthropic shares and lending to the company. It reached $273.72 early and then fell the most in the group — the valuation gains and the AWS capex bill sit in the same portfolio.

TSLA · Tesla

$374.41 (-2.43%). It opened near $393 and slid to $370.95 with no company news, following the de-rating of high-multiple growth names.

AVGO · Broadcom

$346.65 (-2.09%). Wednesday's +3.64% was given back as the AI infrastructure complex sold off after Anthropic's open; custom ASIC demand itself has not changed.

AMD · Advanced Micro Devices

$489.19 (-7.72%), having traded down 11% to $470.56 before recovering, and still near $487 after hours. Hunterbrook Capital disclosed a short position alongside a report titled "AMD has its own China problem". The detail is the subject of today's spotlight. AMD had been up 2.8% premarket on Anthropic enthusiasm, so direction changed twice in one session.

MU · Micron

$690.44 (-2.10%). President and CTO Scott DeBoer said 2027 memory supply will be tighter than 2026 — "we don't see it coming into balance with demand really in any time we can actually pick in the future right now". The outlook improved and the stock still gave back its early gain; this was AI hardware profit-taking rather than a memory problem.

TSM · TSMC

$352.09 (-2.90%). It erased an early gain and led the tech sector lower alongside ASML. Foundry demand has not changed, and Nvidia's raised guidance still means more leading-edge wafer orders.

ANT · Anthropic

$97.17 (+17.1%) on its first day, against an $83 offer price. It opened at $111.50, briefly topped a $2tn market value and settled at about $1.75tn fully diluted. More than 846m shares traded, beating the 522m on SpaceX's debut. At roughly 40 times trailing sales, the valuation was on trial from the first print, and its flows will set the direction for large AI names next week.

CRWV · CoreWeave / NBIS · Nebius

CoreWeave closed at $44.91 (-6.10%) and Nebius at $96.92 (-9.39%). Both opened higher on Texas resuming air permits for data centres and on Anthropic enthusiasm, then gave it all back. A blockbuster AI listing should help the neoclouds; they were sold because the money to buy the new stock had to come from somewhere.

PANW · Palo Alto Networks

$361.73 (+6.42%). Fiscal first-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.05 beat the $0.97 consensus on revenue of $3.44bn ($3.31bn expected), and the company raised full-year guidance to $14.52–14.57bn. It also announced a $4.5bn deal for AI startup Applied Compute. It reached $369.26 before trimming gains, one of the few winners on a down day.

WMT · Walmart

-1.0%. Adjusted EPS of $0.65 beat $0.64 and revenue of $188.84bn beat $187.27bn, with full-year sales guidance raised to 4.9–5.2%. The stock fell anyway: the implied fourth-quarter profit lands below expectations, and a beat was not enough to hold the price.

UNH · UnitedHealth

$330.37 (-5.58%), a second day of heavy selling and the biggest drag on the Dow, with an intraday low of $324.95. CMS's proposed "CRUSH" rule — extrapolating Medicare Advantage audit findings across whole contracts back to payment year 2018 — is still being repriced.

DIS · Walt Disney

$120.27 (-1.71%), a day after hitting a 52-week high. Nothing changed in the results or the cost plan; the pullback came with the index.

What to check next

  1. 01
    Rates

    December 8–9 FOMC — 25bp is 95%, the risk is 50bp

    Futures put a quarter-point hike at a 95% probability. The open question is Governor Christopher Waller's suggestion that the Fed may need to 'shock people' with a half-point move. The October minutes confirmed officials see AI as a material source of inflation risk. With the 10-year above 5%, a 50bp outcome hits long-duration mega-cap multiples first.

    December 8–9
  2. 02
    Event

    Anthropic's first week — where did the money come from

    More than 846m shares changed hands on day one, far above the 522m traded on SpaceX's debut. The question for next week is whether money keeps rotating out of incumbent AI names into ANT at a $97.17 close, or whether the listing was a one-day event that simply gave holders an excuse to take profits.

    November 20–25
  3. 03
    Risk

    The fallout from the AMD short report

    The company called the claims a misrepresentation of its practices and relationships. The underlying facts, though, are in AMD's own filings: 22.4% of revenue billed to China and Hong Kong, and firmware updates reaching a blacklisted customer. What moves the price next is whether Commerce or Congress opens a review, and how the 50%-ownership rule is handled when its suspension lapses.

    Ongoing · January 10, 2027
  4. 04
    Policy

    The five-country AI chip licence rule

    The interim final rule in force this week requires licences for advanced AI chips shipped to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil. AMD billed $4.28bn to customers in Singapore in fiscal 2025, more than 10% of sales, so tighter rules mean lower Asia revenue estimates.

    In force · watch the guidance
  5. 05
    Funding

    The cost of financing the AI build-out

    CoreWeave bond yields traded above 16% earlier this month and the stock is down 18% in November. Anthropic's listing gives investors one more way to own AI, but the neoclouds that lease the capacity still pay up for capital. Cheaper credit is what lets the capex cycle show up in prices again.

    Credit spreads this month
  6. 06
    Calendar

    Thanksgiving and thinner liquidity

    US markets are closed November 26 and close early on November 27. Volume drops around the holiday and index moves overshoot; next month's inflation and jobs data are the last inputs before the December FOMC.
