US Markets Daily Report · November 24

Jensen Huang is back at Nvidia, and the AI trade came back with him. The board reinstated him 12 days after his resignation, Nvidia jumped 31.48%, and the Nasdaq rose 6.51% for its best day since April 2025. The S&P 500 gained 4.73% to 7,237.17 to end a seven-session slide, the Dow added 1,447.98 points (3.01%), the SOX climbed 13.98% and the VIX fell 26.04% to 26.54. The indexes are still below their highs, and Anthropic was the one AI name that fell.

The S&P 500 rose 4.73% (+326.78 points) to 7,237.17, ending a seven-session losing streak. It had been 11.6% below its Oct. 6 record close of 7,818.93; that gap is now 7.4%, enough to leave correction territory. The Nasdaq jumped 6.51% (+1,471.83 points) to 24,089.56 for its best day since April 9, 2025 and its second-largest point gain ever, and it is back in positive territory for the year. The Dow added 1,447.98 points (3.01%) to 49,595.31, the Nasdaq-100 rose 6.86% to 26,746.1 and the Russell 2000 gained around 3.2%.

Chips were the center of it. The PHLX Semiconductor Index climbed 13.98% (+1,175.64 points) to 9,584.23, one of its five best days since 1994 and enough to erase Friday's 10.73% slide in one session. It is still 34.6% below its June 22 record of 14,655.29. The index peaked between 9,713 and 9,728 in the session and gave back part of the gain in the afternoon.

The trigger came before the bell. Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive 12 days after he resigned, a unanimous vote taken, the board said, after receiving input from senior levels of the U.S. government and with the conclusion that his return poses no national security risk. President Trump told CNBC he had pressed the board directly and indirectly, and that Huang did nothing wrong. Nvidia had lost a third of its value within three days of the Nov. 8 disclosure about his affairs, and closed Friday on a seventh straight weekly decline, the longest since 2008. All of that was reversed in a single session.

Nvidia closed at $165.05 (+31.48%), its best day since May 2003, after trading as high as $170.55. Its market value rose by roughly $980 billion, bringing it back near $4 trillion. It still sits below the $173.73 close of the day before Huang resigned and about 21% below where it traded a month ago. This was not an earnings or order-book move; it was a restoration of confidence in management.

Ten of eleven S&P 500 sectors rose. Technology led at 9.5%, followed by communication services at 5.7%, consumer discretionary at 4.8% and industrials at 4.1%. Energy was the only decliner at -1.4%. At one point 433 of 503 index members were higher as buyers returned to the names that had been sold all last week. After the bell, Agilent rose 7% in extended trading on guidance above consensus.

Bonds and currencies moved the other way. The 10-year Treasury yield finished at 4.92% (+0.8bp) after touching 4.984%, the 2-year rose 4.9bp to 4.662% and the 30-year slipped 0.7bp to 5.295%. A $69 billion two-year auction drew bids of 2.65 times the amount offered. Money leaving havens was the simple explanation.

Commodities split. WTI fell 3.8% to $80.25 a barrel, its lowest since August, and Brent dropped 3.6% to $86.68 on hopes of de-escalation with Iran. Gold rose 3.4% to $3,635 an ounce after hitting a 14-month low on Friday. Bitcoin traded near $61,300 after falling below $58,000 on Friday. The dollar index rose 0.5% to 104.3, a 19-month high.

Korea closed on Friday's US session, before New York turned. The KOSPI fell 3.31% to 4,708.54, a 10-month low, after trading as low as 4,586.80 (-5.81%) and triggering a sell-side sidecar for a second straight day. Samsung Electronics fell 3.06% and SK hynix 2.52% while foreigners sold a net 2.47 trillion won. The government began preparing to use the market stabilization fund. The won closed at 1,345.9 per dollar (+9.7) after touching 1,351.6. November semiconductor exports through the 20th reached $38.5 billion, up 294.4% from a year earlier and a record, which together with the US rebound makes for a friendlier Korean open on the 24th.

NVDA · Nvidia

$165.05 (+31.48%), with a range of $156.97 to $170.55. Ratings followed the announcement. Melius Research upgraded to buy with a $220 target, Bernstein to outperform with $200, and StoneX to buy with $270, implying 59% to 115% upside from Friday's close of $125.53. Two investigations remain: the board's independent committee, advised by Wachtell Lipton, and the SEC's look at its accounting. This was a reflex rebound; the next checkpoints are the hearings now being prepared on Capitol Hill and whatever the committee reports.

MSFT · Microsoft

$492.50 (+1.65%). It opened at $505.97 and gave most of that back by the close, finishing at the bottom of the M7 with Apple. There was no company news. A rally that removes an executive risk does little for the company that spends the most on its own infrastructure.

AAPL · Apple

$349.08 (+1.90%). The smallest gain among mega-caps. Apple sat out the AI capex debate on the way down last week and sat out the rebound on the way up. The margin pressure from rising memory prices is still the live issue.

GOOGL · Alphabet

$282.10 (+6.34%). Alphabet was the company behind Friday's chip-selling report, but this time it recovered most of what it lost. The read in the market is that shifting orders to MediaTek does not shrink Alphabet's total spend, and its $10 billion allocation in Anthropic's offering turns into a gain when AI sentiment improves.

META · Meta Platforms

$597.60 (+6.55%), after touching $602.27 intraday. Meta spends the most on its own models and data centers, so it fell hardest last week and bounced hardest here. Broadcom starts shipping Meta's MTIA inference chip in the December quarter, which adds to capex rather than subtracting from it.

AMZN · Amazon

$255.39 (+3.47%). It opened at $260 and slid to $255. Its Anthropic stake and loans are a mark-to-market gain, but AWS spending is what investors keep discounting.

TSLA · Tesla

$385.46 (+11.46%), after trading up to $393.34. Two catalysts landed together: the weekend rollout of Full Self-Driving v15 and renewed speculation that SpaceX could acquire Tesla in an all-stock deal. Tesla says v15 handles safety-critical cases 25 times better and replaces the old wireframe display with a live camera rendering. JPMorgan's Rajat Gupta called it further evidence of Tesla's lead in consumer autonomy, and HSBC raised its rating and more than doubled its target. Against that, NHTSA is investigating about 2.9 million FSD-equipped vehicles, and the stock trades near 175 times forward earnings.

AVGO · Broadcom

$313.55 (+3.00%), the weakest of the chip majors. It opened at $323.06, fell to $305.31 and closed at $313. Broadcom has yet to answer the report that Google cut 2027 TPU 8i orders by 20%, and its exposure of up to $42 billion in Anthropic lending is unchanged. Its Dec. 9 fiscal fourth-quarter report is where it answers. The full picture ran in the Nov. 21 spotlight.

AMD · Advanced Micro Devices

$453.46 (+6.85%). It opened at $467.60 and gave back half the gain in the afternoon. AMD is still working off the short-seller report, and a 7% move on a day the SOX rose 14% reads as relative weakness.

MU · Micron

$707.70 (+19.04%), after trading as high as $719.43. Memory had its best session on record. The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) rose 18-19% for its best day since its April listing, SK hynix's ADR gained 20.6%, Western Digital and Seagate rose 14% and SanDisk 11%. Micron is still 41.7% below its June 25 record close of $1,213.56. Nothing company-specific drove it: the names that were hit hardest when confidence in AI capex broke were the ones that rallied most when it came back. The full picture runs in this issue's spotlight.

TSM · TSMC

$363.37 (+14.01%). A rebound after Taipei's worst week since 1990. Nothing suggested foundry demand had broken, and TSMC remains the first place a report about AI chip orders lands.

INTC · Intel

$59.66 (+16.34%), among the best of the large caps. Foundry spending worries and slow external customer wins eased for a day, taking the stock from the low $50s back to $60. It is still in the lower part of its 52-week range of $32.89 to $142.35.

MRVL · Marvell / Arm · Arm

Marvell closed at $144.74 (+18.37%) and Arm rose about 16%. Custom silicon and IP design led the rebound, helped by the view that Google is taking more MediaTek-designed 8t volume.

CRWV · CoreWeave / NBIS · Nebius / DELL · Dell

CoreWeave closed at $48.33 (+22.32%), Nebius rose about 20%, Dell gained 20.82% to $335.89 and Oracle rose around 12%. Over the past month CoreWeave had fallen 13%, Nebius 37% and IREN 40% on doubts about Nvidia financing its customers. With the author of that structure back, the sellers turned buyers. Nvidia disclosed $36 billion in off-balance-sheet backstops for neoclouds in its July filing.

ANT · Anthropic

$87.64 (-1.43%), effectively the only AI-linked name to fall. It traded as low as $84.79 before paring the loss. After touching $115 on its first day and falling 8.5% on Friday, this was a third day of adjustment, and it sits 5.6% above its $83 offer price. Falling on a day when every other AI name surged is the signal: the market is treating Anthropic as a company that has to prove its revenue forecasts, not as a hardware-cycle beneficiary.

CRWD · CrowdStrike / ZS · Zscaler

CrowdStrike fell around 9%, the worst performer in the S&P 500, while its target Zscaler rose 16-18%. CrowdStrike agreed to buy Zscaler in an all-stock deal the companies valued at about $43.9 billion. Zscaler holders get 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per share, or $260.41, a 34% premium to Friday's close. Combined, the two expect roughly $10 billion in annual recurring revenue. The acquirer's stock fell, which is the usual response and held even on a day like this.

BA · Boeing

Up about 4% after President Trump told CNBC he expects China to buy another 250 Boeing jets, on top of the 200 agreed at the May summit.

UNH · UnitedHealth / DIS · Walt Disney

UnitedHealth fell 1.17% to $335.87 and Disney rose 2.00% to $120.34. The only defensive in the index was where profit-taking showed up on a 5% day. Disney simply followed the risk-on move. Gold's rebound lifted Newmont and Gold Fields more than 7%, copper strength took Freeport-McMoRan up nearly 8%, and International Paper fell 4% on its plan to spin off and list its EMEA packaging business as DS Smith.

What to check next

  1. 01
    Rates

    Oct. PCE on Nov. 25 is the last number before the December decision

    The funds rate has sat at 3.75-4.00%, and the next call is Dec. 8-9. The October minutes showed almost every participant expected further increases, and futures now price a 25bp move almost fully while bets on 50bp have faded. October PCE lands on the 25th as the last inflation print before the meeting. Core PCE is forecast at 3.2% year over year, or 3.3% by RBC, up from 3.1% in September. A print at 3.3% or higher reopens the 50bp argument; 3.1% or lower could pull forward the end of the hiking cycle.

    Nov. 25, 8:30 a.m. ET · FOMC Dec. 8-9
  2. 02
    Earnings

    Broadcom on Dec. 9, Micron on Dec. 17 — the tests for this rally

    This move came from sentiment, not numbers, so guidance will decide what follows. Broadcom reports fiscal Q4 on the 9th against its own guide of $34.8 billion in revenue and $21.7 billion in AI semis, and it is the company that has to answer the report that Google cut 2027 TPU 8i orders. Micron reports on the 17th and needs to confirm $61.5 billion in revenue and $38.15 in EPS. Dell, HP, Best Buy and Deere report this week.

    Dec. 9 and Dec. 17, after the close
  3. 03
    Regulation

    What Huang's return settled, and what it left open

    The board said the independent committee's investigation, run by Wachtell Lipton, will proceed to conclusion, and Huang has agreed to cooperate. The New York Times reported that the woman at the center of the affair was in contact with an officer of China's Ministry of State Security. Lawmakers are preparing hearings on export controls and national security. The stock recovered; the investigations and the hearings did not go away.

    Ongoing · watch for hearings and filings
  4. 04
    Flows

    A holiday tape made a 6.5% day — next week is the real test

    Markets are closed on the 26th and shut at 1 p.m. ET on the 27th. Thin volume moves indexes further than the news does. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky called this a rally back toward 7,250-7,350 before a likely turn lower, and all three indexes remain below their 200-day averages. The condition for the rebound to hold is volume drying up as selling is absorbed, with the VIX settling rather than spiking again.

    Nov. 26-27 · first week of December
  5. 05
    Korea

    Seoul is still trading Friday's close

    Korean markets closed on Friday's US rout before New York reversed. The KOSPI fell 3.31% to 4,708.54, a 10-month low, traded as low as 4,586.80 and triggered a second straight sell-side sidecar. Samsung Electronics fell 3.06% and SK hynix 2.52% while foreigners sold a net 2.47 trillion won. The government has begun preparing to deploy the market stabilization fund. The won closed at 1,345.9 per dollar after touching 1,351.6.

    Korean open, Nov. 24
  6. 06
    Credit

    The funding line under AI capex is still tight

    The biggest winners were neoclouds and servers: CoreWeave 22.32%, Dell 20.82%, Oracle around 12%. These were the first names sold over the past month as doubts grew about Nvidia financing its own customers. Nvidia disclosed $36 billion in off-balance-sheet backstops for neoclouds in its July quarterly filing. One executive's return does not change funding costs or the accounting questions, so credit spreads and private AI credit terms stay on the checklist.
