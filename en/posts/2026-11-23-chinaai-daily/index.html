China AI Daily Report · November 23

A trio of support measures over the weekend and a rebound in US semis during Asian hours turned the A-share session into a V. Shanghai rose 0.15%, ChiNext 3.08%, and more than 4,600 names advanced, ending a seven-day run of mass declines. The two optical-module names each traded over CNY 20bn, while the STAR 50 slipped 0.26% as SMIC, Cambricon and Hygon stayed under pressure.

Including the Beijing exchange the total was CNY 1.564tn (+CNY 132.8bn). Three of the four largest traded values were optical modules or DRAM, and two of our eleven names, Zhongji Innolight and Eoptolink, each cleared CNY 20bn.

The Shanghai Composite closed at 3,538.95 (+0.15%), the Shenzhen Component at 10,454.37 (+1.93%) and ChiNext at 2,280.20 (+3.08%). The morning looked nothing like that. Shanghai opened 0.86% lower at 3,503.52 and slid to 3,476.65, the first time below 3,500 since July 2025. Buyers met the index there, the market chopped sideways through the morning and then turned up in the afternoon to finish green. More than 4,600 names advanced, 146 hit the daily limit (24 of them broke it), and a seven-day streak of more than 4,000 decliners came to an end.

Two things drove the reversal. The first was a policy trio announced over the weekend: with CSRC approval, the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges will lower the minimum margin requirement from 100% to 80% from November 30 — enough collateral for CNY 1m to support CNY 1.25m of stock purchases, or close to CNY 400bn of theoretical buying power. Alongside it came Shenzhen's plan for a market-maker system on ChiNext and exchange fee cuts. The second was the overnight rebound in US semiconductors during Asian hours: after the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 10.73% on Friday, Nvidia traded more than 7% higher and the 3x leveraged semiconductor ETF rose nearly 15%. Together they turned an opening rout into an afternoon recovery.

Sector moves read like a rotation. Film and media, agriculture, humanoid robots and precious metals rose; oil services, telecoms, insurers and baijiu fell. Unitree Robotics hit the 20% limit and roughly 50 humanoid-robot names followed. Money that had hidden in defensives on Friday came straight back out: PetroChina fell more than 5%, while China Telecom, Kweichow Moutai, China Pacific Insurance and Ping An each lost more than 3%. The SSE 50 fell 0.86% and the CSI 300 gained just 0.09% while ChiNext rose 3.08% — that gap is the day in one line.

In AI hardware, the standouts were PCBs and optical modules. The Global Electronics Association's November 18 report, "The PCB Squeeze: a warning for the global electronics industry," flagged bottlenecks running into the first half of 2027. Add a report that Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are asking 25–45% more for AI-server orders already booked, and the PCB theme exploded: Zhongjing Electronics made a third straight limit-up, with Ruifeng Hi-Tech, Aoshikang, Shiyun Circuit and Chongda Technology joining it. In optics, Tianfu Communication rose 13.82%, Eoptolink 9.92% and Zhongji Innolight 6.10%. Inside the STAR board the temperature differed sharply: the STAR Composite gained 2.62% while the STAR 50 fell 0.26%. The 616 STAR names averaged +4.24% with an average swing of 9.61%, while the index heavyweights Cambricon, Hygon and SMIC stood still or slipped.

One caveat is worth stating. Monday's rebound was built from policy and flows, not from a resolution of the doubts about the AI capex cycle. Peppers Global's Woody Lin called official intervention "a circuit breaker rather than a turning point." In the US, the odds of a December 9 rate hike are still above 90%, and the pressure from Treasury yields on long-duration valuations has not eased. Even on a green day, only eight of our eleven names rose, and the large AI names appeared in the turnover tables without moving their share prices.

688256 · Cambricon

CNY 914.80 (−0.57%). It gapped down to 901.11, rallied to 932.90 in the morning, then slid back to 875.90 — a 6.2% intraday range. Turnover was CNY 9.730bn, fifth in the market, and the market cap stands at CNY 575.3bn. No standalone disclosure was identified for the day. The selloff in chip designers that began with the November 16 US export-control update — now requiring BIS authorisation for advanced-node foundry work — is still in charge of short-term flows, while the domestic-substitution argument keeps pulling the other way.

688041 · Hygon

CNY 164.00 (−0.15%). It fell to 157.01, essentially touching the 52-week low of 156.05, then recovered almost the entire loss. That makes three consecutive declines for a cumulative −13.27%. Turnover was CNY 4.939bn and the market cap CNY 381.1bn. The company has disclosed no further purchases under its CNY 500–800m buyback since the first CNY 235m tranche on November 19. With the price pinned near the bottom of its purchase range (CNY 162.69–174.99), the pace of the remaining buying is the near-term support marker. The structural picture is unchanged from our November 19 feature → Hygon spotlight.

300308 · Zhongji Innolight

CNY 417.20 (+6.10%). The stock reached 425.00 intraday and closed with a market value around CNY 490bn. Turnover of CNY 22.767bn was the highest in the entire A-share market and one of only two above CNY 20bn. In the morning the board approved a CNY 2–4bn buyback at a ceiling of CNY 600 a share over three months, for employee incentives — the plan chairman Liu Sheng proposed on November 18. The gap between the price the company is willing to pay and the price the market gives it is the subject of today's feature → Zhongji Innolight spotlight.

300502 · Eoptolink

CNY 181.36 (+9.92%). From 165.00 it ran to 183.60 intraday. Turnover of CNY 20.561bn ranked second and the market cap is around CNY 253bn. The near-10% move partly reflects the delayed effect of the company's November 18 statement: demand guidance is clear, the order book is full, there is "no downward revision to demand expectations or order cancellations," and 1.6T has entered mass production. A CNY 800m–1bn buyback approved on October 23 had already spent CNY 800m (4.216m shares, average CNY 189.70) by November 18, which has helped put a floor under the stock.

688981 · SMIC

CNY 121.60 (−3.08%). The first decline after four straight sessions and a cumulative +17.87%; the stock touched 117.55 intraday. Turnover was CNY 7.363bn and the market cap remains above CNY 1tn. The foundry-substitution case is intact, but an 18% run in four days invited profit-taking first. The STAR 50's −0.26% also comes down to the weight of SMIC, Cambricon and Hygon. The BIS rule on advanced-node foundry approvals cuts both ways: it raises the scarcity value of domestic fabs while narrowing their overseas customer routes.

600183 · Shengyi Technology

CNY 60.75 (+4.08%). It dipped to 56.66 and then climbed back to close near its high of 61.00. Turnover was CNY 5.978bn and the market cap CNY 147.6bn. The move was more about the industry than the company. After the Global Electronics Association warning, data circulated showing standard FR-4 CCL lead times stretching from 2–3 weeks to 6–8 weeks and advanced low-loss grades to 14–18 weeks, with laminate makers raising prices in succession. The company's own numbers support the story: Q3 net profit was CNY 3.440bn, up 62% QoQ, and nine-month profit reached CNY 6.727bn (+175%). Talk of 15% increases on M6-and-below laminates and 25% on prepreg from November is still industry chatter, not a confirmed company figure.

688072 · Piotech

CNY 508.30 (−3.73%). It opened higher at 522.72 and then fell to 488.64. Turnover of CNY 3.483bn was the smallest of our eleven. The day's news was arguably positive: the CSRC approved the CNY 2.5bn acquisition of Wuxi Shangji Semiconductor via a share issue, and chairman Lü Guangquan proposed another CNY 200–300m buyback. The stock still could not escape the equipment-sector pullback. Nine-month revenue was CNY 5.220bn (+23.70%), but Q3 revenue grew only 1.84% year on year and operating cash flow was −CNY 35.6m. The catalysts piled up; the numbers lagged.

603986 · GigaDevice

CNY 214.61 (+4.24%). Turnover of CNY 7.183bn ranked 11th in the market and the market cap is CNY 151.1bn. Rising memory prices showed up directly in results: Q3 revenue of CNY 8.732bn (+225.65%) and net profit of CNY 4.209bn (+728.93%), with nine-month revenue at CNY 20.297bn and profit at CNY 11.066bn. At the November 17 results briefing management said large-capacity NOR prices would keep rising modestly, the SLC NAND shortage would persist "for a considerable period," and niche DRAM would hold at high levels. It also noted that Q4 is a seasonal lull.

688008 · Montage Technology

CNY 132.20 (+2.95%). Turnover was CNY 4.093bn and the market cap CNY 161.4bn. The catalyst was AMD: the company agreed with AMD's China team to deepen cooperation across memory interface, PCIe interconnect, CXL memory expansion and pooling, and clocking, and said its key interconnect chips have completed interoperability validation on AMD's 6th-gen EPYC platform. Its Hong Kong line rose 3.55% to HKD 143.10. Q3 revenue was CNY 2.079bn (+46.0%) and net profit CNY 1.240bn (+162.0%), both records, with CNY 2.50–2.79bn of interconnect orders scheduled for Q4 delivery.

688521 · VeriSilicon

CNY 79.58 (+3.49%). It fell to 73.00 early, extending a seven-session slide to −34.47%, then reversed and closed higher — a recovery of nearly 9% off the low. Turnover of CNY 2.225bn was the smallest in our group and the market cap is CNY 41.9bn. The bounce looks more like short covering and bargain hunting in a heavily sold name than fresh money arriving. The medium-term drivers — design-IP wins and the wearable IP subsystem launched on November 19 — are unchanged.

688825 · CXMT

CNY 35.90 (+2.10%). It set a new 52-week low at 33.61 and then reversed, with a 6.63% range. Turnover of CNY 10.965bn was the highest on the STAR board and third in the market. The market cap of CNY 2.44tn rose by CNY 50.2bn on the day. Flows went the other way: main funds were net sellers for a second session (CNY 1.937bn over two days) and CNY 3.147bn over five sessions. A November 18 visit by the chairman of China Chengtong, a state capital operating company, to discuss semiconductor industry support remains a medium-term item.

What to Watch Next

  1. 01
    Policy

    The first week of an 80% margin floor

    The Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges will cut the minimum margin requirement from 100% to 80% starting November 30. The theoretical figure is about CNY 400bn of extra buying power, but the proof sits in margin balances and new account openings, plus whatever maintenance ratios brokers set on top. Monday's jump priced the announcement; next week shows whether the money actually arrives.

    Nov 30 to early December
  2. 02
    Flows

    CNY 43.3bn crammed into two optical names

    Zhongji Innolight (CNY 22.767bn) and Eoptolink (CNY 20.561bn) together took 2.8% of the whole market's turnover. The other side of that coin matters: Zhongji's margin financing balance fell for two straight sessions to CNY 14.880bn by November 20. While the company supports the stock with its own cash, leveraged holders have been leaving — so whether turnover holds up on November 24–26 is the tell.

    November 24–26
  3. 03
    Disclosure

    What a CNY 600 cap on the buyback means

    The board approved CNY 2–4bn of purchases on November 23, with a ceiling of CNY 600 a share, 44% above Monday's CNY 417.20 close. At the cap that is up to 6.66m shares, or 0.57% of the register, within three months. The plan follows the 12.69% slide on November 3 over order-cut talk, so the pace of execution is itself a measure of management's confidence.

    Execution filings within three months
  4. 04
    Results

    Whether 25–45% AI-server price hikes reach components

    One catalyst on Monday was a report that Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are telling customers to pay 25–45% more on orders already signed, citing memory costs and board shortages. Higher server prices leave room for optical modules and PCBs to negotiate, but they can also slow Q4 deliveries and push revenue recognition out. December pricing talks will settle which effect dominates.

    December to January
  5. 05
    Risk

    The 2.9-point gap between STAR 50 and the STAR Composite

    Inside the same board, the top 50 by market value fell while the other 616 names averaged +4.24%. Cambricon, Hygon and SMIC dragged the index down while robotics and AI-application small caps ran. If the rebound does not spread to the large AI names, the bounce in domestic compute may be a one-day rotation. Turnover recovery in those three names is the test.

    This week
  6. 06
    Policy

    The first BIS advanced-node foundry approval case

    The US export-control update of November 16 requires BIS authorisation for advanced-node foundry work, with designers needing approval before a fab can accept the order. That rule drove the November 19 selloff in domestic AI chips. It lifts domestic substitution demand while narrowing overseas manufacturing routes; only the first approval cases and their processing time will show the real effect.

    Weeks after taking effect
